# PSYCHO SALES

## Canonical Psychological Knowledge System for Sales

PSYCHO SALES is an **ontology-first, scientifically grounded psychological knowledge system** for sales, persuasion, buyer behavior, negotiation, objection and resistance, relationships, salesperson performance, scientific models, and integrated sales intelligence.

It is designed from the beginning for two audiences:

1. **Humans** — including readers with weak English who need immediate clarity.
2. **Language models** — which require precise, bounded, stable, independently retrievable knowledge.

---

# CORE PRINCIPLE

> **Simple language. Deep thinking.**

The language must be extremely easy.

The psychology must be extremely deep.

Easy English must never be achieved by removing important mechanisms, conditions, relationships, evidence limits, or boundaries.

---

# MASTER BUILD LAW

> **Architecture → Ontology → Ownership Audit → Lock → Knowledge Writing → Validation → Versioning**

This order is mandatory.

We do not build thousands of knowledge objects on top of an unstable ontology.

---

# THE MOST IMPORTANT LAW

# ONE CONCEPT = ONE CANONICAL OWNER

Every important concept has one canonical owner.

A concept may be:

- referenced elsewhere;
- applied elsewhere;
- used in examples;
- connected to many other concepts;
- analyzed by the intelligence layer.

But its canonical meaning belongs to one owner.

### Example

**System 1:** Motivation

**System 6:** Buyer Motivation

**System 9:** Sales Motivation

System 6 and System 9 may explain how motivation operates in their own domains.

They do not become the canonical owner of general motivation.

# MASTER SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## SYSTEM 1 — HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY

General psychological mechanisms underlying:

- thought;
- emotion;
- motivation;
- perception;
- learning;
- judgment;
- decision-making;
- behavior;
- social functioning.

**Role:** Foundational psychology layer.

---

## SYSTEM 2 — COLD CALLING PSYCHOLOGY

Psychology specific to initiating unsolicited or minimally anticipated sales contact.

**Role:** Owns the psychological situation created by cold outreach.

---

## SYSTEM 3 — COMMUNICATION PSYCHOLOGY

Psychological processes governing interpersonal communication.

**Role:** Owns communication mechanisms.

---

## SYSTEM 4 — PERSUASION PSYCHOLOGY

Psychological processes through which messages, sources, contexts, and social forces influence attitudes, judgments, intentions, and behavior.

**Role:** Owns general persuasion and persuasion-specific resistance.

---

## SYSTEM 5 — NEGOTIATION PSYCHOLOGY

Psychological processes involved when parties with partially aligned and partially competing interests exchange information, manage conflict, bargain, and seek agreement.

**Role:** Owns negotiation-specific preparation, interaction, strategies, tactics, ethics, and outcomes.

---

## SYSTEM 6 — BUYER PSYCHOLOGY

Psychological processes specific to buying.

Includes:

- buying behavior;
- buyer motivation;
- needs and wants;
- purchase decisions;
- perceived purchase risk;
- satisfaction;
- post-purchase processes.

**Role:** Buyer-side purchasing domain.

---

## SYSTEM 7 — OBJECTION & RESISTANCE PSYCHOLOGY

Sales-specific psychological mechanisms behind:

- objections;
- hesitation;
- rejection;
- defensiveness;
- barriers to proceeding.

**Role:** Owns sales-specific objection and resistance phenomena.

General persuasion resistance remains owned by System 4.

---

## SYSTEM 8 — RELATIONSHIP PSYCHOLOGY

Psychological processes governing sustained buyer–seller relationships.

Includes:

- formation;
- trust;
- commitment;
- maintenance;
- deterioration;
- repair;
- loyalty;
- exit.

**Role:** Long-term commercial relationship psychology.

---

## SYSTEM 9 — SALES PERFORMANCE PSYCHOLOGY

Psychology of the salesperson as a performer.

Includes:

- motivation;
- self-regulation;
- discipline;
- confidence;
- attention;
- stress;
- resilience;
- habits;
- learning;
- performance behavior.

**Role:** Salesperson-as-performer.

---

## SYSTEM 10 — SCIENTIFIC THEORIES, MODELS & FRAMEWORKS

Scientific:

- theories;
- models;
- frameworks;
- research methods;
- formal explanatory structures.

**Role:** Scientific synthesis layer.

System 10 does not become the owner of the underlying psychological constructs.

---

## SYSTEM 11 — PSYCHO SALES INTELLIGENCE

Integrated reasoning over Systems 1–10.

Includes:

- knowledge representation;
- concept relationships;
- psychological diagnosis;
- state modeling;
- context analysis;
- multi-mechanism reasoning;
- evidence handling;
- scenario reasoning;
- knowledge retrieval;
- sales decision support.

**Role:** Intelligence and integration layer.

System 11 does not create new psychological owners.

---

# SYSTEM / DOMAIN BOUNDARY LAW

Every system must clearly answer:

1. What does it own?
2. What does it not own?
3. Which systems does it interact with?
4. Where does ownership stop?

A system cannot be locked while these boundaries remain unclear.

---

# APPLICATION ≠ OWNERSHIP

A later system may use knowledge from an earlier system.

Examples:

- Cold calling uses attention.
- Persuasion uses cognition and emotion.
- Buyer psychology uses motivation and decision-making.
- Sales performance uses self-regulation and learning.
- Intelligence combines concepts from every system.

Using a concept does not create a second owner.

---

# SCIENTIFIC MODEL RULE

A theory or model explaining a construct is not automatically the owner of that construct.

**System 10 owns the model.**

The underlying psychological construct remains with its canonical owner.

---

# INTELLIGENCE LAYER RULE

System 11 can combine knowledge.

It can reason across concepts.

It can diagnose situations.

It can model states.

It can retrieve relevant knowledge.

It cannot silently create a new psychological owner.

---

# CANONICAL WRITING STANDARD

Every major knowledge object normally contains:

1. **Purpose**
2. **Definition**
3. **Core Understanding**
4. **Mechanism**
5. **Conditions**
6. **Effects**
7. **Relationship to This Chapter**
8. **Related Concepts**
9. **Boundaries**
10. **Examples**
11. **Scientific Status**
12. **Key Principles**

The structure can change when scientifically necessary.

Sections must never be added only to make the article longer.

---

# WRITING STYLE

## Language

Use:

- very simple English;
- short sentences;
- familiar words;
- direct explanations;
- one idea at a time;
- clear cause → process → effect structure.

Avoid:

- unnecessary academic jargon;
- decorative language;
- inflated vocabulary;
- vague claims;
- bloated sentences;
- repetition.

### The target

A person with weak English should understand the first meaning at first glance.

A psychologically knowledgeable reader should still find deep mechanisms underneath.

An LLM should be able to retrieve the object and identify its exact meaning.

---

# DEPTH STANDARD

For major concepts, explain where relevant:

- what it is;
- why it exists;
- what causes it;
- how it works;
- what changes it;
- what it affects;
- how it interacts with other mechanisms;
- what it does not mean;
- where its boundaries are;
- why it matters.

**Length is not depth.**

Mechanism is depth.

---

# CUMULATIVE TEACHING

The preferred progression is:

> **Definition → Mechanism → Conditions → Interaction → Consequence → Application**

Every paragraph must add new understanding.

Do not repeat one idea using different words.

---

# SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Never invent:

- studies;
- statistics;
- citations;
- experiments;
- mechanisms;
- scientific consensus.

Clearly distinguish:

- established findings;
- strong empirical evidence;
- theoretical explanations;
- empirical observations;
- practical interpretations;
- contested findings;
- unresolved questions.

Uncertainty must remain visible when uncertainty exists.

---

# EXAMPLE RULE

Examples must clarify the concept.

They must not:

- redefine the concept;
- introduce another concept as part of the definition;
- create a false scientific claim.

Examples should demonstrate mechanisms.

---

# CROSS-SYSTEM REFERENCE RULE

When a concept depends on another concept:

1. name it;
2. explain only what is needed;
3. identify the relationship;
4. point toward its canonical owner;
5. stop before taking over its explanation.

This protects canonical ownership.

---

# LLM TRAINING STANDARD

Every knowledge object must be useful when retrieved independently.

The object should make clear:

- what the concept is;
- what it means;
- how it works;
- what causes or changes it;
- what it affects;
- what it relates to;
- who owns it;
- what it is not;
- how strong the evidence is.

Do not optimize for keyword stuffing.

Optimize for **semantic clarity and stable meaning**.

---

# RETRIEVAL STABILITY

Terminology must remain stable.

If two concepts differ, keep them separate.

If two terms are synonyms, explain their relationship instead of creating unnecessary duplicates.

Canonical names should remain stable across:

- files;
- chapters;
- examples;
- references;
- system indexes.

---

# KNOWLEDGE OBJECT INDEPENDENCE

A knowledge object should remain understandable when retrieved without the surrounding chapter.

Dependencies must be explicit.

Hidden context must not be required to understand the core meaning.

---

# SALES APPLICATION RULE

PSYCHO SALES can explain practical sales techniques and applications.

But:

> **Practical usefulness is not scientific proof.**

A sales technique may be useful without being a universal psychological law.

Separate:

**psychological mechanism**

from

**sales practice**

from

**sales folklore**.

---

# ETHICS

The encyclopedia may describe:

- persuasion;
- influence;
- pressure;
- resistance;
- tactics;
- manipulation mechanisms.

Description is not endorsement.

Where ethical boundaries matter, they should be explicit.

---

# QUALITY GATE

Every canonical knowledge object must pass:

**Clarity + Depth + Ownership + Boundaries + Scientific Integrity + Retrieval Stability**

A beautiful explanation with bad ownership fails.

A scientifically accurate explanation that a beginner cannot understand also fails.

---

# COMPLETION TEST

Before accepting a topic:

1. Can a beginner explain what it means?
2. Can the reader explain how it works?
3. Can the reader identify important conditions?
4. Can the reader distinguish it from nearby concepts?
5. Is the canonical owner clear?
6. Has it avoided stealing another concept's ownership?
7. Does every major paragraph add new understanding?
8. Is scientific confidence represented honestly?
9. Is it independently retrievable?
10. Does it belong in this exact chapter and system?

A critical failure requires revision.

---

# ONTOLOGY AUDIT

Before a system is locked:

- audit every chapter;
- audit every topic;
- compare neighboring topics;
- compare earlier systems;
- search for duplicate owners;
- search for renamed duplicates;
- search for overly broad topics;
- verify boundaries;
- verify terminology.

Ownership conflicts must be resolved before knowledge generation.

---

# LOCKING RULE

A system becomes **LOCKED** only after:

1. chapter ontology is complete;
2. topic ontology is complete;
3. ownership is audited;
4. cross-system conflicts are checked;
5. boundaries are documented;
6. terminology is reviewed;
7. completion tests pass.

Locking means controlled stability.

It does not mean the system can never improve when evidence requires change.

---

# VERSIONING

Changes must be traceable.

Major ontology changes require:

- version information;
- reason for change;
- affected concepts;
- ownership impact;
- validation.

Never silently change a canonical definition when the change affects meaning.

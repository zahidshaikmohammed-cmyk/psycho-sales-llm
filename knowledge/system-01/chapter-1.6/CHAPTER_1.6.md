## 1.6.1 Attention

## 1. Purpose
Selective allocation of processing priority among available information or tasks..

## 2. Definition
Attention is the attentional phenomenon or process described by its role in selecting, maintaining, shifting, or regulating information processing.

## 3. Core Understanding
Attention is not a single mechanism; selection can be guided by goals, stimulus properties, location, objects, features, task demands, and internal states.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
This Topic belongs to Chapter 1.6 because it describes attention or a direct attentional operation, condition, or failure within the architecture of information selection.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
Owns the general attentional construct; defers perception, memory, consciousness, and broader executive-function mechanisms to their canonical chapters.

### Defers
It does not re-own neighboring psychological constructs that have their own canonical Topics elsewhere in System 1.

## 6. Key Principles
- Attention is selective and context-sensitive rather than unlimited.
- Attentional effects depend on task demands, goals, stimulus properties, and state.
- The Topic should not be treated as an absolute mechanism outside the conditions in which it is defined.

## 1.4.1 Sensation

## 1. Purpose
This Topic establishes the meaning, operation, and boundaries of Sensation within the science of sensation and perception. It exists to preserve a precise distinction between this perceptual construct and neighboring concepts.

## 2. Definition
the process by which sensory systems detect stimulation and convert it into information available for neural processing.

**What it is not:** Sensation should not be treated as synonymous with every related sensory or perceptual process; its specific scope is defined above.

## 3. Core Understanding
Sensation concerns detection and registration. Receptors respond selectively to physical or chemical stimulation; transduction converts that stimulation into neural activity; coding represents properties such as intensity, timing, and location. Sensory detection is probabilistic and depends on stimulus conditions, background activity, adaptation, and observer state.

### Evidence Status
The construct is established within psychological science at the level stated here. Specific mechanisms, effects, and boundary conditions vary by modality, task, population, and evidence base; claims beyond the scope stated here should not be inferred.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Perception is the later organization and interpretation of sensory information.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
The general concept of sensory detection and registration..

### Defers
sensory receptors, transduction, coding, and perception.

## 6. Key Principles
- Sensation concerns sensory registration; it is not identical to perception or conscious awareness.
- Interpretation depends on the relevant sensory system, task, and conditions.
- Related concepts must remain at their canonical owners rather than being re-explained here.

# Chapter-Level Ownership Notes

Chapter 1.4 owns universal principles of sensation and perception. It does not re-own broader biological, cognitive, developmental, social, clinical, or methodological constructs merely because they influence perception.

The following ownership boundaries are intentional:

- **Interoceptive perception** is the perceptual expression of the broader **Interoception** construct owned by Chapter 1.3.
- **Visual pathways** is the modality-specific visual pathway object in this chapter; broader **Sensory pathways** remains owned by Chapter 1.3.
- **Bottom-up processing** and **Top-down processing** concern perception; **Bottom-up attention** and **Top-down attention** remain owned by Chapter 1.6.
- **Perceptual learning** concerns learning-specific changes in perception; general **Learning** remains owned by Chapter 1.7.
- **Continuity** concerns perceptual grouping; Chapter 1.15 uses **Developmental continuity**, which concerns developmental change.
- **Context effects** concern perceptual context; Chapter 1.20 uses **Treatment context effects**, which concerns intervention settings.
- **Individual differences in perception** concerns variation within perception; broader **Individual differences** remains owned by Chapter 1.1.
- No sales framework is used to define universal perceptual science. Any downstream application must derive from these established psychological constructs without changing their meaning.

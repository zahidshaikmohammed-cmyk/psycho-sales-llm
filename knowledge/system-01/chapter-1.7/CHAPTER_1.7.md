## 1.7.1 Learning

## 1. Purpose
Explain relatively enduring change produced by experience.

## 2. Definition
A relatively enduring change in knowledge, behavior, skill, or behavioral potential produced by experience.

## 3. Core Understanding
Learning must be distinguished from temporary performance changes caused by fatigue, motivation, arousal, maturation, or immediate state. It may involve associations, knowledge, strategies, skills, or behavioral tendencies.

### Mechanisms
Learning includes conditioning, observation, cognitive acquisition, habituation, sensitization, skill practice, and habit formation. No single mechanism explains every form.

### Constraints
What is learned depends on prior experience, biological preparedness, task structure, consequences, context, and opportunity to express the acquired capability.

### Additional depth
Learning is best identified by change attributable to experience rather than by a single successful performance. Persistence, transfer, context sensitivity, and expression conditions help separate acquired capacity from transient motivation or state effects.

## 1.7.3 Behavioral change through learning

## 1. Purpose
Explain how learning produces durable changes in behavior.

## 2. Definition
A relatively enduring alteration in the probability, form, timing, or effectiveness of behavior resulting from experience.

## 3. Core Understanding
Learning can increase, suppress, redirect, or refine behavior. Classical conditioning changes responses to predictive stimuli; operant learning changes behavior through consequences.

### Performance distinction
A temporary behavioral change does not establish learning. Stable change across relevant conditions provides stronger evidence.

### Maintenance
Behavior learned under one contingency can weaken when cues, consequences, or contexts change. Maintenance therefore depends on continuing learning conditions and competing learning.

### Additional depth
Behavioral change is evidence for learning only when plausible alternatives such as maturation, fatigue, arousal, opportunity, or immediate incentives are controlled. Learned behavior can be suppressed or expressed differently without the underlying acquisition being erased.

Operant conditioning concerns changes in behavior related to its consequences. The functional effect of a consequence depends on whether it changes future behavior, not on whether the event is inherently rewarding or aversive.

# 1.1.37 — Dynamic Interaction

## 1. Purpose
Dynamic interaction explains psychological functioning as continuously changing relationships among processes, states, people, and environments rather than as isolated variables.

## 2. Definition
Dynamic interaction is the ongoing mutual influence among psychological components or between a person and surrounding conditions across time.

## 3. Core Understanding
Attention can alter memory encoding; memory can alter later attention. Emotion can change interpretation; interpretation can change emotion. Behavior can change social conditions, which alter future behavior. Such loops create time-dependent patterns.

Dynamic interaction does not mean chaotic or unpredictable behavior. It means that the current state partly depends on prior states and ongoing interactions.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
It integrates systems, feedback, reciprocal causation, temporal dynamics, and person–environment relationships.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- General dynamic interaction as an architectural principle.
### Defers
- Dynamic-systems models → theory registry.
- Specific interactions → canonical domain chapters.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Psychological components influence one another over time.
2. Current functioning can depend on prior states and feedback.
3. Dynamic interaction is broader than one causal pathway.
4. Dynamic does not mean random.
5. Specific mechanisms require evidence.

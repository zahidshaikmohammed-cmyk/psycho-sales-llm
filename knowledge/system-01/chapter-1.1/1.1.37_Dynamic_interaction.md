# 1.1.37 — Dynamic Interaction

## 1. Purpose
Dynamic interaction explains psychological functioning as continuously changing relationships among processes, states, people, and environments rather than as isolated variables.

## 2. Definition
Dynamic interaction is the ongoing mutual influence among psychological components or between a person and surrounding conditions across time.

## 3. Core Understanding

Dynamic interaction means that psychological components influence one another while their own states are changing. It differs from a simple list of correlations because time, sequence, feedback, and changing conditions are part of the explanation.

Attention may alter what enters memory; remembered information may then guide future attention. Emotion can influence interpretation; interpretation can change emotion. A behavior can alter another person's response, which changes the social environment and affects later behavior.

Dynamic interaction can produce stability. Repeated feedback can maintain a pattern even when the original trigger is no longer present. It can also produce change when a new input, learning event, or environmental shift moves the system toward a different pattern.

Dynamic does not mean unpredictable. Some dynamic systems produce highly regular trajectories. Nor does it mean every psychological process is nonlinear. The term should be used when interaction across time is genuinely important.

Research on dynamic interaction often benefits from repeated measurement because a single snapshot can hide the sequence through which the pattern developed.

1. Psychological components influence one another over time.
2. Current functioning can depend on prior states and feedback.
3. Dynamic interaction is broader than one causal pathway.
4. Dynamic does not mean random.
5. Specific mechanisms require evidence.

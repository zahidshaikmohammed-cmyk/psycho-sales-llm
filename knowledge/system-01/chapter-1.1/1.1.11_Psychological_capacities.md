# 1.1.11 — Psychological Capacities

## 1. Purpose

Psychological capacities describe what a person or psychological system is able to do, rather than what it is doing at one moment. They are needed to distinguish potential or ability from current performance, behavior, state, or trait.

## 2. Definition

A psychological capacity is a capability of a person or psychological system to perform, support, learn, regulate, represent, or adapt in a particular way under suitable conditions. Capacity concerns potential functioning, not guaranteed performance.

## 3. Core Understanding

Capacity is conditional. A person may have the capacity to remember information but perform poorly when exhausted, distracted, or highly stressed. Performance therefore provides evidence about capacity but does not perfectly reveal it.

Capacities can be broad or specific. Attention, language, learning, reasoning, emotional regulation, social understanding, and motor coordination involve different capacities. Capacities can develop through maturation and learning, be constrained by biology, and vary across individuals.

Capacity should be distinguished from skill. A skill is an acquired ability to perform a task; capacity is the broader potential that can support performance. Capacity should also be distinguished from trait: traits describe relatively stable tendencies, while capacities describe what the system can accomplish.

Capacity is often measured indirectly through performance under defined conditions. A single failed performance does not prove absence of capacity, and a successful performance does not prove that performance will generalize to all contexts.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Capacities connect person, individual differences, psychological functions, learning, adaptation, and performance. They provide the foundation for discussing what people can do without equating ability with behavior.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Capacity is potential functioning, not guaranteed performance.
2. Performance depends on capacity and conditions.
3. Capacities can develop and change.
4. A single observation cannot fully establish a person's capacity.
5. Capacity and skill are related but not identical.

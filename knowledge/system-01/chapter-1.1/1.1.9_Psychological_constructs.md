# 1.1.1 — Psychological Constructs

A psychological construct is an abstract concept used to represent a psychological attribute, process, capacity, state, or pattern that cannot necessarily be observed directly in its entirety. Constructs allow psychology to study concepts such as intelligence, anxiety, motivation, self-control, memory ability, and personality traits without pretending that the label itself is directly visible.

## 1. Purpose

Psychological constructs are essential because much of psychology studies abstract phenomena. This Topic establishes how such concepts function scientifically and prevents constructs from being confused with measurements or observations.

## 2. Definition

A psychological construct is a theoretically meaningful concept used to represent a psychological phenomenon that is not reducible to one direct observation. A construct gains scientific usefulness through clear definition, relationships to observable indicators, and evidence supporting the way it is measured and interpreted.

## 3. Core Understanding

A construct is not the same as an operational definition. The construct is the concept; an operationalization specifies how researchers measure or manipulate it in a particular study. One construct can have several valid measures, and one measure can sometimes capture more than one construct.

Constructs vary in abstraction. Some are broad, such as intelligence or personality. Others are narrower, such as working-memory capacity or response inhibition. A good construct has conceptual boundaries that distinguish it from neighboring concepts.

Construct validity is central. Researchers must ask whether the evidence supports interpreting a measure as representing the intended construct. Evidence can include relationships with related measures, distinction from different constructs, internal structure, predictive relationships, and responses to experimental manipulation.

Constructs should not be treated as hidden objects that automatically cause behavior. A construct may summarize a pattern of functioning rather than identify a single underlying mechanism. Mechanistic explanation requires additional evidence.

Constructs are part of the architecture's basic ontology. They connect abstract psychological knowledge with measurement, research, and later specialized domains.

## 6. Key Principles

1. A construct is an abstract scientific concept, not a direct observation.
2. Measurement operationalizes a construct; it does not become the construct.
3. Constructs require clear conceptual boundaries.
4. Construct validity concerns whether evidence supports the intended interpretation.
5. A construct label is not automatically a causal mechanism.

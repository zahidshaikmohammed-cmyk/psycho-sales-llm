# 1.1.52 — Structural Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Structural explanation describes how psychological components are organized and related.

## 2. Definition
A structural explanation accounts for a phenomenon through the organization, components, and relationships within a psychological system.

## 3. Core Understanding

Structural explanation focuses on organization: components, relationships, hierarchy, and relatively stable arrangement. In psychology, structures can be conceptual, functional, cognitive, social, or biological depending on the domain.

A structural account can identify how a system is organized without fully explaining how it changes. For example, identifying components of working memory describes architecture, while explaining how information is maintained describes process.

Structural and process explanations are complementary. Structure can constrain what processes are possible, while repeated processes can change organization over time.

Structural claims should be supported by evidence. Researchers should distinguish an inferred latent structure from a directly observed object and recognize that alternative structural models can sometimes fit the same data.


## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Structural explanation connects psychological organization and systems to later process and mechanism explanations.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- Structural explanation as an architectural level.
### Defers

## 6. Key Principles
1. Structural explanation describes organization.
2. Structure does not automatically explain process.
3. Structural and process accounts can complement each other.

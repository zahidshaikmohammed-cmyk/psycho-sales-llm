# 1.1.54 — Mechanistic Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Mechanistic explanation identifies the organized processes that produce or regulate a phenomenon. It is the most direct foundation for asking how psychological effects occur.

## 2. Definition
A mechanistic explanation accounts for an outcome by specifying the components and operations whose interaction generates, maintains, or changes it.

## 3. Core Understanding

Mechanistic explanation goes beyond saying that two things are related. It identifies the components and operations that connect a condition to an outcome. In psychology, mechanisms can involve attention, learning, appraisal, memory, motivation, regulation, social interaction, or biological processes.

A mechanism can contain multiple interacting parts. For example, goal pursuit may involve goal activation, monitoring, effort allocation, feedback, and adjustment. No single step necessarily explains the whole pattern.

Mechanistic claims are strongest when interventions or manipulations change the proposed mechanism and produce predicted changes in the outcome. Correlational evidence can be informative but may not establish the pathway.

Mechanisms can be context-sensitive and probabilistic. The same mechanism may produce different outcomes when competing processes or environmental constraints change.


## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Mechanistic explanation is the explanatory counterpart of psychological mechanisms and connects all later domains.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Mechanistic explanation specifies how an outcome is generated.
2. Mechanisms require evidence.
3. Mechanisms can operate across levels.
4. Mechanistic accounts can include interacting components.

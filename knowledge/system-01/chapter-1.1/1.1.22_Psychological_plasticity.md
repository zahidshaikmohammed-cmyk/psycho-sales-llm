# 1.1.22 — Psychological Plasticity

## 1. Purpose

Plasticity establishes that psychological functioning can change through experience, development, learning, and environmental conditions. It prevents stable psychological characteristics from being treated as permanently fixed.

## 2. Definition

Psychological plasticity is the capacity of psychological functioning to change in response to experience, learning, development, context, or intervention.

## 3. Core Understanding

Plasticity varies across processes and timescales. Some abilities change substantially with training, while others show greater stability. Plasticity is also constrained by biology, prior development, resources, age, and environmental conditions.

Plasticity does not mean unlimited change. The ability to change does not imply that any characteristic can be transformed into any desired form. Evidence must establish the size, durability, and conditions of change.

Plasticity can be adaptive or maladaptive. Learning can improve skills, but repeated exposure to harmful environments can also establish costly patterns. Change itself is not evidence of improvement.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Plasticity connects change, adaptation, development, learning, stability, flexibility, and person–environment interaction.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Psychological functioning can change.
2. Plasticity is real but constrained.
3. The amount and durability of change vary by characteristic and condition.
4. Plasticity can produce beneficial or harmful patterns.
5. Change should be established empirically rather than assumed from the existence of plasticity.

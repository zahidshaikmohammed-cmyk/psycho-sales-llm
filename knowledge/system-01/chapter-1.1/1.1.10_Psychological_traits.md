# 1.1.1 — Psychological Traits

A psychological trait is a relatively enduring individual difference in characteristic patterns of psychological functioning. Traits help describe why people differ in typical behavior, emotion, cognition, motivation, or interpersonal style across situations and time.

## 1. Purpose

Traits are foundational because psychology needs to distinguish relatively stable individual differences from temporary states and situation-specific responses.

## 2. Definition

A psychological trait is a relatively stable characteristic that differs among individuals and is expressed as a tendency toward particular patterns of psychological functioning across relevant situations and time. Traits are probabilistic tendencies, not fixed behavioral rules.

## 3. Core Understanding

Trait concepts summarize regularities. A person high on a particular trait is not expected to behave identically in every situation. Situational demands, goals, learning history, current states, and other traits can alter expression.

Traits can concern personality, cognitive abilities, temperament, motivational tendencies, or other individual differences. Their scientific value depends on reliable measurement and evidence that the trait predicts meaningful patterns.

Traits should be distinguished from states. A state is a current condition; a trait is a relatively enduring tendency. The distinction is not absolute because traits can change over development and repeated states can contribute to longer-term patterns.

The person-situation relationship is central. Trait expression depends partly on situations. A trait can be stable while behavior varies because different situations activate different aspects of the person.

Traits can be measured through self-report, informant report, behavioral indicators, performance tasks, or combinations. Measurement error and context can affect apparent stability.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Traits provide the individual-difference layer of the foundation. They connect person-level stability with situational variation and later personality science.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Traits describe tendencies, not guaranteed behavior.
2. Traits are relatively stable, not permanently fixed.
3. Trait expression depends on context.
4. Traits are individual differences that require evidence for reliable measurement.
5. A trait is not the same as a temporary state or a single behavior.

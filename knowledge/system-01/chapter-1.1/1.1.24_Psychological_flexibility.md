# 1.1.24 — Psychological Flexibility

## 1. Purpose

Flexibility describes the ability to adjust psychological processing and behavior when circumstances change. It is important because effective functioning often requires changing strategies rather than repeating one response.

## 2. Definition

Psychological flexibility is the capacity to alter psychological processes, responses, goals, or strategies in ways that appropriately fit changing internal or environmental conditions.

## 3. Core Understanding

Flexibility is not simply changing frequently. Appropriate flexibility requires sensitivity to conditions and the ability to maintain useful goals while adjusting methods. Excessive switching without stability can be as ineffective as rigid repetition.

Flexibility can involve shifting attention, revising beliefs, changing strategies, regulating emotion, updating goals, or changing behavior when consequences indicate that a response is no longer effective.

Flexibility depends on available skills and resources. Stress, fatigue, uncertainty, rigid beliefs, strong habits, or limited alternatives can reduce flexibility.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Flexibility connects plasticity, adaptation, regulation, behavior, action, and person–environment interaction.

## 5. Boundaries

### Owns
- General flexibility as adaptive adjustment.

### Defers

- Cognitive flexibility → 1.9.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Flexibility means appropriate adjustment, not constant change.
2. Flexibility and stability can coexist.
3. Effective flexibility depends on context and available alternatives.
4. Rigidity can limit adaptation, but persistence can also be useful.
5. Flexibility is a capacity, not a guarantee of effective behavior.

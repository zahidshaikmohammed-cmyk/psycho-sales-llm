# 1.1.50 — Dynamic-Systems Perspective

## 1. Purpose
The dynamic-systems perspective provides a formal way to understand psychological change as patterns emerging from interacting components over time.

## 2. Definition
The dynamic-systems perspective treats psychological functioning as a changing system in which multiple interacting processes influence trajectories, stability, transitions, and adaptation.

## 3. Core Understanding
Dynamic systems can show stable patterns, variability, feedback, nonlinear change, and transitions between states. Small changes in conditions can sometimes produce larger changes when the system is near a transition.

The perspective is valuable when timing and interaction matter. It does not mean that every psychological phenomenon is nonlinear or unpredictable.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
It connects systems, temporal dynamics, emergence, reciprocal causation, stability, plasticity, and change.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- General dynamic-systems perspective.
### Defers
- Developmental applications → 1.15.
- Specific dynamic models → their canonical domains.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Psychological systems can change through interacting processes over time.
2. Stable patterns and transitions can coexist.
3. Feedback can shape trajectories.
4. Dynamic-systems models require evidence appropriate to temporal processes.

# 1.1.50 — Dynamic-Systems Perspective

## 1. Purpose
The dynamic-systems perspective provides a formal way to understand psychological change as patterns emerging from interacting components over time.

## 2. Definition
The dynamic-systems perspective treats psychological functioning as a changing system in which multiple interacting processes influence trajectories, stability, transitions, and adaptation.

## 3. Core Understanding

Dynamic-systems thinking emphasizes trajectories rather than isolated measurements. It is useful when psychological patterns emerge from multiple interacting processes and change over time.

A dynamic system can show stable patterns, variability, feedback, gradual change, sudden transitions, and dependence on prior conditions. These properties can appear in development, learning, emotion, motor behavior, and social interaction.

The perspective does not imply that all psychology is nonlinear or impossible to predict. Some systems are relatively stable and regular. Dynamic analysis becomes useful when timing, interaction, and changing relationships are central to the phenomenon.

Dynamic models require data capable of capturing change. A single cross-sectional measurement usually cannot test claims about trajectories or reciprocal change.

3. Feedback can shape trajectories.
4. Dynamic-systems models require evidence appropriate to temporal processes.

# 1.1.19 — Person–Environment Interaction

## 1. Purpose

People do not simply receive environmental influence; they perceive, select, modify, and respond to environments. Person–environment interaction provides a broader framework than the immediate situation and explains how individuals and environments continuously shape one another.

## 2. Definition

Person–environment interaction is the reciprocal relationship between characteristics of a person and features of the broader physical, social, cultural, and institutional environment in which the person develops and functions.

## 3. Core Understanding

Environment includes more than immediate surroundings. It can include family, school, workplace, community, culture, institutions, physical resources, social networks, and material conditions.

People respond differently to the same environment because they differ in needs, capacities, histories, identities, and perceptions. People also influence environments by choosing relationships, changing routines, creating social structures, and producing consequences that alter later conditions.

Development makes this interaction especially important. Environments provide opportunities and constraints, while people increasingly gain capacities to select or modify aspects of their environment. The relationship is therefore dynamic across time.

Person–environment interaction should not be confused with simple environmental determinism. Environment can strongly constrain behavior, but psychological and biological systems mediate how conditions are experienced and responded to.

This concept connects person, situation, context, culture, development, adaptation, behavior, and reciprocal causation.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Environments influence psychological functioning.
2. People also select, interpret, and modify environments.
3. Person–environment relationships change over time.
4. Environmental influence is not identical to deterministic control.
5. Development and adaptation are partly shaped by person–environment interaction.

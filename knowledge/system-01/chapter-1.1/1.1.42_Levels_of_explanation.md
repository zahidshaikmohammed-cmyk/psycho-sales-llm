# 1.1.42 — Levels of Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Levels of explanation clarify what kind of account is being offered and prevent one explanatory level from being treated as the only legitimate one.

## 2. Definition
A level of explanation is the conceptual scale and type of causal or functional account used to explain a psychological phenomenon.

## 3. Core Understanding

An explanatory level answers a different kind of "why" question. A biological account may explain how neural or hormonal activity contributes. A psychological account may explain information processing or motivation. A social account may explain interaction or group influence. A cultural account may explain shared meaning and practice.

These levels can be linked. A social condition can influence psychological appraisal, which can affect physiological regulation and behavior. A biological constraint can alter psychological processing, which can change social interaction.

The mistake is not using one level; it is treating one level as sufficient by default. The appropriate level depends on the phenomenon, question, evidence, and desired prediction.

Explanatory levels should also be distinguished from levels of analysis. They are related but not identical: one concerns where the phenomenon is studied, while the other concerns the type of explanation being offered.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Explanations can operate at different levels.
2. One level does not automatically invalidate another.
3. Explanatory level should match the question.
4. Multilevel explanations require clear relationships among levels.

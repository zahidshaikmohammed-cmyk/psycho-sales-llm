# 1.1.34 — Feedback

## 1. Purpose
Feedback explains how information about outcomes or current states can influence later psychological processing and behavior. It is essential for learning, regulation, adaptation, and dynamic systems.

## 2. Definition
Feedback is information generated by the consequences or current state of a system that can influence subsequent processing, behavior, or regulation.

## 3. Core Understanding

Feedback occurs when information about current conditions or consequences becomes available to the system and can influence what happens next. It is central to regulation because effective adjustment requires some information about whether the current state or behavior is moving toward or away from a relevant target.

Feedback can be internal or external. Internal feedback can include awareness of bodily state, error signals, or perceived progress. External feedback can include another person's reaction, task performance, environmental consequences, or explicit information about an outcome.

Feedback can stabilize a system or change it. Negative feedback in the systems sense reduces deviation from a target; positive feedback can amplify a change. These terms should not be confused with positive and negative reinforcement.

Feedback only becomes useful when the system can detect, interpret, and act on it. A person may receive accurate information but ignore it, misunderstand it, lack the ability to respond, or have competing goals. Feedback is therefore an input to regulation, not a guarantee of improvement.

Feedback can also become distorted. Delayed consequences, noisy environments, ambiguous social signals, and inaccurate self-monitoring can make it difficult to identify what caused an outcome. Psychological systems often have to learn from incomplete feedback.

Repeated feedback can change behavior and internal representations. Practice can use performance feedback to improve a skill. Social interaction can provide feedback about norms or relationship quality. Self-regulation can compare current performance with a standard and adjust future behavior.


## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Feedback links behavior, action, learning, self-regulation, adaptation, and reciprocal causation.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Feedback provides information about current or resulting states.
2. Feedback can stabilize or change a system.
3. Feedback is not automatically corrective.
4. Interpretation determines how feedback is used.
5. Feedback is central to regulation and adaptation.

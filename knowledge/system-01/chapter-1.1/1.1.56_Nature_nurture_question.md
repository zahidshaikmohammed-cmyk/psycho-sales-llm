# 1.1.56 — Nature–Nurture Question

## 1. Purpose
The nature–nurture question addresses how biological inheritance and experience contribute to psychological development and individual differences. It is foundational because modern psychology must avoid treating biology and environment as mutually exclusive causes.

## 2. Definition
The nature–nurture question concerns the relative and interacting contributions of biological inheritance and environmental experience to psychological characteristics and behavior.

## 3. Core Understanding

The nature–nurture question is now better understood as an interaction problem than a simple competition between genes and environment. Biological inheritance can influence development and sensitivity, while environments influence behavior, learning, physiology, and gene expression.

Heritability is often misunderstood. It describes the proportion of variation in a characteristic within a particular population and environment that is statistically associated with genetic differences. It does not describe how much of one person's characteristic is genetic and does not establish immutability.

Environmental effects can also vary across people. Individuals may select different environments, respond differently to the same conditions, or evoke different responses from others. These processes create gene–environment and person–environment interactions.

The important scientific question is therefore not whether nature or nurture "wins," but which biological and experiential processes contribute, under which conditions, and through what mechanisms.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Nature and nurture usually interact.
2. Heritability is a population statistic, not an individual's genetic percentage.
3. Genetic influence does not imply immutability.
4. Environmental influence does not imply unlimited malleability.
5. Development reflects interacting biological and experiential processes.

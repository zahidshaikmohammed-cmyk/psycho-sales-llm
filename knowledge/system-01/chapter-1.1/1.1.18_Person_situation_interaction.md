# 1.1.18 — Person–Situation Interaction

## 1. Purpose

Psychological behavior often reflects both who a person is and what situation they are in. Person–situation interaction provides the foundation for explaining why traits do not produce identical behavior everywhere and why situations do not affect everyone identically.

## 2. Definition

Person–situation interaction is the process by which characteristics of a person and characteristics of a situation jointly influence psychological processing and behavior, such that the effect of one depends partly on the other.

## 3. Core Understanding

An interaction means that the relationship is conditional. A situation may strongly affect one person and weakly affect another, or a person's characteristic may become visible only in particular situations.

Interactions can occur through perception. People do not encounter situations as completely neutral observers; they interpret cues according to knowledge, goals, expectations, and prior experience. Those interpretations can influence which responses are considered possible.

People can also choose situations. A person may seek supportive environments, avoid threatening ones, or create social conditions through behavior. This creates reciprocal relationships rather than a simple one-way effect from situation to person.

Person–situation interaction does not mean that personality or situations never have independent effects. Both can contribute directly, while their combination creates additional variation.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

This concept connects person, situation, context, behavior, traits, states, and reciprocal causation. It is a central bridge between individual differences and environmental influence.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Person and situation can jointly influence behavior.
2. Situational effects can depend on person characteristics.
3. Person characteristics can be expressed differently across situations.
4. People can select and modify situations.
5. Person–situation interaction is not a claim that either person or situation is irrelevant alone.

# 1.1.28 — Information Selection

## 1. Purpose

Information selection explains why people do not process every available signal equally. It is foundational to attention and broader psychological architecture because selection determines what receives limited processing resources.

## 2. Definition

Information selection is the process by which a psychological system prioritizes some available information for enhanced processing while reducing, delaying, or excluding other information.

## 3. Core Understanding

Selection can be driven by goals, physical salience, novelty, emotional relevance, learned importance, or environmental demands. It can occur before, during, or after different stages of processing depending on the task and theoretical model.

Selection is not absolute filtering. Information that is not the current focus can sometimes influence behavior or enter awareness, and unexpected events can capture attention. The system therefore balances goal-directed control with sensitivity to important changes.

Selection can be spatial, feature-based, object-based, temporal, or task-based. It can also involve choosing among competing actions or interpretations.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Information selection is an architectural bridge to attention, perception, memory, decision-making, and response selection.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Psychological systems selectively process information.
2. Selection can be goal-driven or stimulus-driven.
3. Unselected information is not necessarily completely excluded.
4. Selection depends on context, goals, salience, and capacity.
5. Selection shapes later processing and behavior.

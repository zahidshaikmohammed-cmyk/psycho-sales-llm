# 1.1.55 — State–Trait–Process Distinction

## 1. Purpose
This distinction prevents three different levels of psychological description from being confused: current conditions, enduring tendencies, and ongoing operations.

## 2. Definition
A state is a current psychological condition; a trait is a relatively enduring individual difference; a process is an operation or change occurring over time.

## 3. Core Understanding
A person can have an anxious trait, experience anxiety as a current state, and engage in threat appraisal as a process. These can influence one another without being identical.

The categories are conceptually useful rather than absolute natural boxes. Some constructs can be described at more than one level depending on the research question.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
This distinction organizes mental states, traits, processes, stability, change, and individual differences.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- Foundational state–trait–process distinction.
### Defers
- Specific traits, states, and processes → canonical chapters.

## 6. Key Principles
1. State, trait, and process describe different psychological levels.
2. A trait can influence states and processes.
3. Processes can change states.
4. The categories should not be treated as rigid universal boxes.

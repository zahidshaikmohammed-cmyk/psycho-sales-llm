# 1.1.17 — Context

## 1. Purpose

Context explains why psychological events cannot always be understood from the immediate situation alone. Broader social, cultural, developmental, historical, institutional, and environmental conditions can change what a situation means and what responses are possible.

## 2. Definition

Context is the broader set of conditions surrounding a psychological phenomenon that influence its meaning, occurrence, expression, interpretation, or consequences. Context can include social, cultural, developmental, historical, physical, institutional, and temporal conditions.

## 3. Core Understanding

Context operates at multiple scales. A conversation has an immediate context; the relationship between the speakers provides a broader social context; cultural norms and institutional rules can provide wider context. These layers can interact.

Context can alter expression without changing the underlying construct. The same emotion may be expressed differently across cultures or social settings. The same ability may be easier to demonstrate under supportive conditions than under threat or resource scarcity.

Context is not merely background information. It can be part of the causal structure. It can change available choices, attention, incentives, social expectations, and consequences.

Context should not become an explanation that means "everything around it." Good analysis specifies which contextual factor matters, how it operates, and what evidence supports its influence.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Context extends the immediate situation across broader levels. It connects person, situation, environment, culture, development, and social systems.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Psychological phenomena occur within context.
2. Context can alter meaning, expression, opportunity, and consequences.
3. Context operates at multiple levels.
4. Context should be specified rather than used as a vague explanation.
5. Broader context can interact with immediate situations and person-level characteristics.

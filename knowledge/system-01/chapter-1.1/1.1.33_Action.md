# 1.1.33 — Action

## 1. Purpose
Action is a central bridge between psychological organization and behavior. It captures organized responding directed toward outcomes while remaining distinct from the broader concept of behavior.

## 2. Definition
An action is an organized behavioral event or sequence that is directed toward, or functionally related to, an outcome, goal, or task. The term often implies greater organization or intentionality than the broader term behavior, although exact usage varies by theory.

## 3. Core Understanding
Actions can be deliberate or partly automatic. Complex action may involve intention, selection, initiation, execution, monitoring, and correction. A single action can contain many simpler movements and behaviors.

Action is closely related to agency, but action does not always imply full conscious control. Learned actions can become highly automatic. An action can also be interrupted or altered by environmental changes.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Action connects response selection, behavior, feedback, regulation, and adaptation. Chapter 1.21 owns detailed action architecture.

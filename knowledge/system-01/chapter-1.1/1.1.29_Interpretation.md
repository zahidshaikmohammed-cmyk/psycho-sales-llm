# 1.1.29 — Interpretation

## 1. Purpose

Interpretation is the process through which information is given psychological meaning. It is essential because the same event can lead to different responses depending on how it is understood.

## 2. Definition

Interpretation is the process of assigning, constructing, or updating psychological meaning from available information, prior knowledge, context, goals, and expectations.

## 3. Core Understanding

Interpretation is not arbitrary. It is constrained by available information and learned knowledge, but it can differ because people attend to different cues or hold different expectations.

Interpretation can concern physical events, social signals, language, internal sensations, or one's own behavior. It can be rapid and automatic or deliberate and reflective.

Interpretation can be revised when new evidence arrives. However, prior beliefs can sometimes make revision difficult. This is one reason perception, memory, reasoning, and decision-making can be influenced by expectations.

An interpretation is not the same as a fact. Scientific analysis should distinguish observed information from the meaning assigned to it.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Interpretation connects information selection with representation, evaluation, mental contents, behavior, and context.

## 5. Boundaries

## 6. Key Principles

1. Interpretation gives information psychological meaning.
2. Interpretation is constrained but not always uniquely determined by input.
3. Prior knowledge and expectations influence interpretation.
4. Interpretations can change with new evidence.
5. An interpretation is not automatically an objective fact.

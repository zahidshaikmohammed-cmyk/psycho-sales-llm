# 1.1.25 — Psychological Change

## 1. Purpose

Psychological change explains how characteristics, processes, states, and behavioral patterns become different across time. It is necessary for understanding development, learning, adaptation, intervention, and recovery.

## 2. Definition

Psychological change is a measurable alteration in psychological functioning across time, including changes in states, processes, capacities, traits, representations, behavior, or organization.

## 3. Core Understanding

Change can be gradual or rapid, intentional or unintentional, reversible or relatively enduring. It can occur through learning, development, biological change, social experience, environmental shifts, stress, or intervention.

Change should be distinguished from measurement noise. A difference between two observations is not automatically genuine psychological change. Reliable measurement and appropriate time scales are needed.

Change can occur at different levels. A person may learn a new skill without major personality change, or repeated experiences may gradually alter beliefs and traits. Different components can therefore change at different rates.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Change connects plasticity, stability, development, learning, adaptation, and dynamic interaction.

## 5. Boundaries

### Owns
- General concept of psychological change.
- Distinction between change and stability.

### Defers

## 6. Key Principles

1. Psychological characteristics can change over time.
2. Change can differ in speed, durability, and direction.
3. Not every observed difference represents real change.
4. Different psychological components can change at different rates.
5. Change can be adaptive, neutral, or harmful.

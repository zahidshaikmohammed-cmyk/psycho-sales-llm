# 1.1.49 — Systems Perspective

## 1. Purpose
The systems perspective emphasizes relationships, feedback, organization, and interactions among components rather than isolated variables.

## 2. Definition
A systems perspective views psychological functioning as organized interaction among interdependent components within nested environments.

## 3. Core Understanding
Systems may contain feedback loops, multiple timescales, and changing relationships. A change in one component can alter conditions for others.

The perspective is useful for complex phenomena but should not replace specific mechanism testing. Saying that something is a system does not by itself explain why it behaves as it does.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
It connects psychological systems, dynamic interaction, reciprocal causation, and emergence.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- Systems perspective as a general explanatory framework.
### Defers
- Dynamic-systems perspective → 1.1.50.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Components can be interdependent.
2. Feedback and relationships matter.
3. Systems can be nested.
4. Systems language does not replace mechanism evidence.

# 1.1.16 — Situation

## 1. Purpose

Human behavior cannot be explained only by characteristics inside the person. Situations create opportunities, demands, cues, constraints, and consequences that shape psychological processing and behavior. This Topic establishes situation as a core unit of analysis.

## 2. Definition

A situation is the set of immediate or proximal conditions in which a person is functioning at a particular time, including relevant physical, social, task, informational, and environmental features.

## 3. Core Understanding

Situations matter because behavior is responsive to conditions. A person may act differently in a private setting, a group, a competitive environment, or a threatening situation even when enduring personal characteristics remain similar.

A situation is not simply a physical location. Its psychological meaning depends partly on how the person perceives and interprets it. The same room can be experienced as safe, threatening, competitive, or supportive by different people.

Situations can contain cues, constraints, affordances, social norms, goals, rewards, costs, and uncertainty. They can activate different psychological processes and make some responses easier or harder.

Situation should not be treated as a universal cause. Person and situation interact. The same situation can produce different responses in different people, and people can actively select, avoid, create, or modify situations.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Situation is paired with person in the person–situation architecture. It also connects to context, environment, behavior, processing, and feedback.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Behavior occurs within situations.
2. Situations provide cues, opportunities, constraints, and consequences.
3. Psychological meaning of a situation depends partly on the person.
4. The same situation can affect people differently.
5. People can also select and change their situations.

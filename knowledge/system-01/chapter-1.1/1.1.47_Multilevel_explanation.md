# 1.1.47 — Multilevel Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Many psychological phenomena involve interacting biological, psychological, social, and cultural conditions. Multilevel explanation provides a framework for integrating these levels without collapsing them into one.

## 2. Definition
A multilevel explanation accounts for a psychological phenomenon using evidence from more than one explanatory level and specifies how the levels relate.

## 3. Core Understanding
A strong multilevel account does more than list factors. It identifies relationships. For example, social stress can alter psychological appraisal, which can influence behavior and physiological regulation.

Not every explanation needs every level. Adding levels without a clear relationship creates complexity without understanding.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Multilevel explanation integrates biological, psychological, social, cultural, and systems perspectives.

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- General architecture for connecting explanatory levels.
### Defers
- Specific mechanisms → relevant domains.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Psychological phenomena can have causes and mechanisms at multiple levels.
2. Levels should be linked, not merely listed.
3. A multilevel model should match the question and evidence.
4. More levels do not automatically produce a better explanation.

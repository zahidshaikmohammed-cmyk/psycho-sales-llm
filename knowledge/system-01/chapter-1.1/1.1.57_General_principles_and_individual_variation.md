# 1.1.57 — General Principles and Individual Variation

## 1. Purpose
Psychology needs both general principles and recognition of variation. This Topic establishes how universal-looking findings should be interpreted without assuming identical functioning in every person.

## 2. Definition
General principles are patterns or relationships that hold across defined populations or conditions; individual variation refers to meaningful differences in how those principles are expressed among people, situations, or contexts.

## 3. Core Understanding
A general effect can coexist with substantial individual variation. A treatment may work on average while some people respond differently. A cognitive bias may be common without occurring identically in every decision. A developmental pattern can be typical without being universal.

Scientific generalization requires specifying the population, conditions, measurement, and uncertainty. A finding from a narrow sample should not automatically be treated as a universal human law.

Individual differences can arise from traits, states, learning histories, biological characteristics, social conditions, culture, and their interactions.

## 6. Key Principles
1. General psychological principles are usually conditional, not universal in every circumstance.
2. Individual variation can coexist with population-level regularities.
3. Generalization requires attention to population and context.
4. Group averages should not be treated as individual predictions.
5. Good psychology explains both regularity and variation.

# 1.1.15 — Person

## 1. Purpose

The concept of person identifies the individual human organism as a central unit of psychological analysis. It prevents explanations from becoming detached lists of processes and establishes where traits, states, capacities, history, goals, and behavior are integrated.

## 2. Definition

A person is an individual human organism considered as an integrated psychological, biological, behavioral, and social system with a history, current state, capacities, relationships, goals, and characteristics that influence functioning.

## 3. Core Understanding

A person is not reducible to one trait or current state. Psychological functioning emerges from interactions among relatively enduring characteristics, current conditions, learned patterns, biological systems, relationships, and environments.

The person has continuity through time but is not psychologically static. Memory, identity, personality, skills, beliefs, relationships, and goals can change while maintaining a sense of personal continuity.

The person is also situated. People act within physical, social, cultural, and institutional environments. Explaining behavior therefore often requires both person-level and situation-level information.

Person should not be treated as a synonym for personality. Personality is one domain of individual functioning. A person also includes cognition, emotion, motivation, development, health, social relationships, and biological characteristics.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Person is one pole of the person–situation and person–environment architecture. It connects individual differences, psychological organization, states, processes, and behavior.

## 6. Key Principles

1. A person is an integrated psychological and biological system.
2. A person includes both stable characteristics and changing states.
3. Person-level functioning cannot be understood independently of context.
4. Personality is one part of the person, not the whole person.
5. Personal continuity can coexist with psychological change.

# 1.1.44 — Psychological Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Psychological explanation describes phenomena through psychological processes, states, representations, mechanisms, and individual differences.

## 2. Definition
A psychological explanation accounts for behavior or mental life using psychological processes or structures and their relationships.

## 3. Core Understanding

Psychological explanation works at the level of processes, states, representations, mechanisms, goals, and individual differences. It can explain why a person interprets information in a particular way, selects one response rather than another, or changes behavior after experience.

Psychological explanation can be mechanistic, functional, structural, or process-based. It can also connect multiple psychological domains. For example, a motivational explanation can incorporate emotion, attention, memory, and decision processes.

Psychological explanation does not deny biological implementation. It provides a level of description that can make predictions about behavior and experience even when the underlying neural details are not fully specified.

The strongest explanations are testable. A psychological label becomes scientifically useful when it leads to predictions that distinguish it from alternatives.


## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
This is the central explanatory level of System 1 and connects the foundation to every later psychological domain.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Psychological explanations describe psychological processes and relations.
2. They can coexist with biological explanations.
3. A psychological label should not be mistaken for a mechanism without evidence.
4. Explanation requires evidence, not plausibility alone.

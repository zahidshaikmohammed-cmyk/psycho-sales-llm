# 1.1.21 — Psychological Adaptation

## 1. Purpose

Adaptation explains how psychological functioning changes in relation to demands, opportunities, and environments. It is foundational because behavior and mental life are not only about immediate reactions; people can adjust across time.

## 2. Definition

Psychological adaptation is the process by which psychological functioning changes, maintains effective organization, or develops strategies in response to recurring or changing internal and environmental demands.

## 3. Core Understanding

Adaptation can involve learning, coping, goal adjustment, skill development, emotional regulation, environmental selection, or changes in interpretation. It does not always mean becoming comfortable or successful. A pattern can be adaptive in one context and costly in another.

Adaptation can occur through deliberate and automatic processes. It can also be individual or social. People adapt to physical conditions, social relationships, developmental transitions, and cultural environments.

Adaptation should not be equated with conformity. A person may adapt by changing the environment, finding a better environment, or resisting a harmful demand. Nor is every change adaptive; change can be ineffective or harmful.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Adaptation links behavior, learning, person–environment interaction, plasticity, flexibility, stability, and change.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Adaptation involves change or maintenance of functioning in relation to demands.
2. Adaptation is context-dependent.
3. Not every change is adaptive.
4. Adaptation can involve changing the person, behavior, interpretation, or environment.
5. Adaptive functioning is not identical to conformity.

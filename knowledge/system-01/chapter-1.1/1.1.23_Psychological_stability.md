# 1.1.23 — Psychological Stability

## 1. Purpose

Stability is necessary to understand continuity in personality, abilities, beliefs, and behavioral patterns. Without it, psychology cannot explain why people remain recognizably similar across time.

## 2. Definition

Psychological stability is the persistence of a psychological characteristic or pattern across time or across relevant conditions, within defined limits.

## 3. Core Understanding

Stability is relative. A trait may show substantial consistency while still changing gradually. A skill may remain stable under familiar conditions but decline under fatigue. A belief may persist despite changing circumstances but change after strong evidence.

Different forms of stability must be distinguished: rank-order stability concerns relative differences among people; mean-level stability concerns average levels; structural stability concerns relationships among components; individual consistency concerns a person's own pattern across situations.

Stability can arise from biological constraints, repeated environments, habits, social reinforcement, identity, or self-maintaining systems. It can also coexist with plasticity.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Stability is paired with plasticity and change. It connects traits, states, organization, behavior, and development.

## 6. Key Principles

1. Stability means persistence, not immutability.
2. Stability can differ across characteristics and timescales.
3. A stable trait can produce variable behavior across situations.
4. Stability and plasticity can coexist.
5. Stability must be measured rather than assumed.

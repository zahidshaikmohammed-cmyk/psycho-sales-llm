# 1.1.38 — Temporal Dynamics

## 1. Purpose
Temporal dynamics explains why psychological phenomena must sometimes be studied across time rather than as single snapshots.

## 2. Definition
Temporal dynamics refers to how psychological states, processes, behavior, and relationships change, persist, fluctuate, and influence one another across time.

## 3. Core Understanding
Different psychological phenomena operate at different timescales. Attention can shift within seconds; emotions can change over minutes; habits can develop over weeks; personality can change over years. A theory must match the timescale of the phenomenon.

Temporal order matters for causal reasoning. If one event occurs after another, that alone does not prove causation, but causal explanations generally require appropriate temporal structure.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Temporal dynamics connects change, stability, feedback, development, adaptation, and reciprocal causation.

3. Stability and fluctuation are both temporal patterns.
4. Temporal order matters for causal explanation.
5. Longitudinal evidence is often needed for change claims.

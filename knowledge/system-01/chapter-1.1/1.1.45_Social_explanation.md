# 1.1.45 — Social Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Social explanation accounts for psychological functioning through interaction with other people, groups, roles, norms, relationships, and social structures.

## 2. Definition
A social explanation explains psychological phenomena through interpersonal and group processes and the social conditions in which people function.

## 3. Core Understanding

Social explanation focuses on how other people and social structures become part of psychological functioning. Interaction can change attention, emotion, identity, motivation, judgment, behavior, and health.

Social influence can be direct, such as a request or social reward, or indirect, such as norms and expectations. Relationships can provide support and resources but can also create conflict, threat, or constraints.

Social explanations should preserve individual variation. People interpret social information differently and bring different histories to the same interaction. Social conditions can also be shaped by the behavior of individuals within them.

Social explanation therefore fits naturally with reciprocal and multilevel models rather than simple social determinism.

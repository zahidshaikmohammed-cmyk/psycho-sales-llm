# 1.1.43 — Biological Explanation

## 1. Purpose
Biological explanation identifies how bodily and biological systems contribute to psychological functioning.

## 2. Definition
A biological explanation accounts for psychological phenomena through biological processes, structures, regulation, development, genetic variation, or organismic systems.

## 3. Core Understanding

Biological explanations can operate at several scales, including genes, cells, neural circuits, endocrine systems, immune processes, and whole-body regulation. Different questions require different biological levels.

Biological findings can establish mechanisms, constraints, developmental influences, or correlates. A brain measurement associated with a psychological process is not automatically a complete explanation of that process. Correlation, causation, and implementation must be distinguished.

Biology and experience interact. Learning can alter neural organization, environments can influence physiological regulation, and genetic differences can change sensitivity to environmental conditions. Therefore, biological explanation does not require biological determinism.

Good biological explanations connect biological evidence to the psychological phenomenon rather than simply naming a brain region or chemical.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Biology contributes to psychological functioning.
2. Biological explanation can involve multiple interacting systems.
3. Biological correlates are not automatically complete explanations.
4. Experience and environment can interact with biological processes.

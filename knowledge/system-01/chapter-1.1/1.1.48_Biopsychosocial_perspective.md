# 1.1.48 — Biopsychosocial Perspective

## 1. Purpose
The biopsychosocial perspective prevents psychological phenomena from being reduced to only biology, only mental processes, or only social conditions.

## 2. Definition
The biopsychosocial perspective is an integrative framework that considers biological, psychological, and social factors as potentially interacting influences on human functioning.

## 3. Core Understanding

The biopsychosocial perspective treats biological, psychological, and social factors as potentially interacting parts of human functioning. It is particularly useful when a phenomenon clearly spans bodily regulation, mental processes, and social conditions.

Biological factors can affect vulnerability and capacity. Psychological processes can influence behavior and interpretation. Social conditions can provide stress, support, resources, and constraints. The relationships among these factors can be reciprocal.

The framework should not become a checklist. Not every phenomenon has equally important biological, psychological, and social explanations, and the perspective itself does not prove a causal relationship.

Its value is architectural: it reminds researchers to consider levels that may otherwise be ignored and then test their relevance rather than assuming them.


## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

## 5. Boundaries
### Owns
- General biopsychosocial framework.
### Defers

## 6. Key Principles
1. Human functioning can involve interacting biological, psychological, and social influences.
2. The framework does not imply equal contribution in every case.
3. Specific relationships require evidence.
4. Integration should improve explanation, not merely increase factors.

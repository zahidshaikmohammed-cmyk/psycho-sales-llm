# 1.1.36 — Reciprocal Causation

## 1. Purpose
Reciprocal causation captures situations in which variables influence one another over time. It prevents psychology from treating person, behavior, and environment as one-way causes.

## 2. Definition
Reciprocal causation is a dynamic causal relationship in which changes in one component can influence another, while changes in the second can subsequently influence the first.

## 3. Core Understanding

Reciprocal causation becomes important when effects feed back into later conditions. A person's behavior can influence other people, and their responses can influence the person's next behavior. Beliefs can affect actions, while action outcomes can provide evidence that changes beliefs. Environments can shape people while people also select and modify environments.

Reciprocity unfolds across time. The fact that two variables are related does not establish that both causally influence each other. A reciprocal explanation requires evidence about temporal order, plausible pathways, and alternative explanations.

Reciprocal relationships can stabilize patterns. A person who expects rejection may behave cautiously, receive a cooler social response, and interpret that response as confirmation. This example illustrates a possible feedback pattern, not a universal mechanism. Other pathways could produce the same observations.

Reciprocal causation also allows change. If a person changes behavior, the environment may respond differently, creating new information and opportunities. This is one reason psychological development and intervention can involve loops rather than one-time causes.

The concept should not be expanded until every variable is described as causing every other variable. Good models specify which pathways are supported and under what conditions.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Causes can become consequences and later causes.
2. Reciprocal relationships unfold over time.
3. Reciprocity must be demonstrated, not assumed.
4. Feedback is a common pathway for reciprocal influence.
5. Reciprocal causation does not mean universal mutual causation.

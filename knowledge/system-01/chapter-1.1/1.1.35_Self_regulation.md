# 1.1.35 — Self-Regulation

## 1. Purpose
Self-regulation is foundational because people often need to alter thoughts, emotions, attention, or behavior to pursue goals under changing conditions.

## 2. Definition
Self-regulation is the process of monitoring and influencing one's psychological states, processes, or behavior in relation to goals, standards, demands, or valued outcomes.

## 3. Core Understanding
Self-regulation can involve goal setting, monitoring, inhibition, strategy selection, emotional regulation, attention control, effort allocation, and adjustment after feedback. It can be deliberate or become partly automatic through learning.

Regulation is not always successful. Limited resources, competing goals, strong habits, stress, fatigue, and environmental barriers can reduce effective control. Self-regulation also depends on what standard or goal is being pursued.

Self-regulation is not identical to self-control. Self-control is one form of regulation involving control over impulses or competing responses; self-regulation is broader.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter
Self-regulation connects goals, feedback, processing, action, adaptation, stability, and change. Chapter 1.12 owns its detailed motivational architecture.

## 6. Key Principles
1. Self-regulation involves monitoring and influencing one's functioning.
2. Regulation is organized around goals, standards, or demands.
3. Self-regulation can succeed or fail depending on conditions.
4. Self-control is narrower than self-regulation.
5. Feedback supports regulation.

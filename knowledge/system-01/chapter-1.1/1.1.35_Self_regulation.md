# 1.1.35 — Self-Regulation

## 1. Purpose
Self-regulation is foundational because people often need to alter thoughts, emotions, attention, or behavior to pursue goals under changing conditions.

## 2. Definition
Self-regulation is the process of monitoring and influencing one's psychological states, processes, or behavior in relation to goals, standards, demands, or valued outcomes.

## 3. Core Understanding

Self-regulation is broader than simply resisting temptation. It includes setting or maintaining goals, monitoring progress, controlling attention, managing emotion, adjusting behavior, allocating effort, and changing strategies when conditions change.

A useful architecture is goal or standard → monitoring → comparison → response or adjustment → new information. This is not a rigid sequence; people can regulate through habits and automatic processes as well as deliberate control.

Effective regulation depends on the goal being pursued. The same behavior can be successful under one standard and unsuccessful under another. Regulation also depends on resources, skills, environmental support, competing goals, and the strength of learned responses.

Self-regulation can fail for many reasons. A person may set unrealistic goals, misjudge progress, fail to notice relevant feedback, choose an ineffective strategy, or face conditions that exceed available capacity. Failure does not by itself demonstrate a lack of motivation or character.

Self-regulation can become more efficient through learning. Repeated routines can reduce the need for deliberate control, while implementation of effective strategies can make goal pursuit easier. Regulation can also involve changing the environment rather than relying only on internal effort.

Self-control is one part of self-regulation. It often concerns controlling impulses or competing responses, whereas self-regulation also includes planning, monitoring, emotion regulation, and strategic adjustment.

2. Regulation is organized around goals, standards, or demands.
3. Self-regulation can succeed or fail depending on conditions.
4. Self-control is narrower than self-regulation.
5. Feedback supports regulation.

# 1.1.26 — Stimulus

## 1. Purpose

A stimulus is a basic term for something that can provide information or occasion a psychological or behavioral response. It is foundational to describing perception, learning, attention, and responding, while avoiding the idea that stimuli automatically produce fixed reactions.

## 2. Definition

A stimulus is an event, object, energy pattern, signal, or condition that can be detected or functionally relevant to an organism and thereby influence psychological processing or behavior.

## 3. Core Understanding

Stimuli can be physical, social, internal, or symbolic. A sound can be a sensory stimulus; another person's facial expression can be a social stimulus; a bodily sensation can provide internal stimulation; a written word can function as a symbolic stimulus.

A stimulus is not identical to its psychological meaning. The same physical event can be interpreted differently depending on attention, learning, expectations, and context. A stimulus may also go unnoticed when attention or sensory conditions limit processing.

Stimuli can be relevant because they provide information, signal consequences, trigger learned associations, or change current demands. In classical conditioning, particular stimuli can acquire learned significance. In perception, sensory stimulation is transformed into organized experience.

## 4. Relationship to This Chapter

Stimulus connects the environment with psychological processing, information selection, interpretation, response selection, and behavior.

## 6. Key Principles

1. A stimulus can influence psychological processing or behavior.
2. Stimuli can be external, internal, social, or symbolic.
3. Stimuli do not automatically produce one fixed response.
4. Psychological significance depends partly on learning and context.
5. Detection and interpretation of a stimulus are distinct processes.

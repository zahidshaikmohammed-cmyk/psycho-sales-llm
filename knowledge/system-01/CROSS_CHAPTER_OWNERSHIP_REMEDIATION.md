## Confirmed Ownership Boundaries

### 1.1.14 Individual differences ↔ 1.3.70 Biological individual differences
- **1.1.14 Individual differences** owns individual variation as a general psychological construct.
- **1.3.70 Biological individual differences** owns biological sources and manifestations of individual variation.
- Boundary: general psychological variation vs biological variation.

### 1.1.22 Psychological plasticity ↔ 1.3.11 Neural plasticity
- **1.1.22 Psychological plasticity** owns the general capacity for psychological change in response to experience and conditions.
- **1.3.11 Neural plasticity** owns biological changes in neural structure, function, connectivity, or excitability.
- Boundary: psychological-level changeability vs neural-level plasticity.

### 1.1.21 Psychological adaptation ↔ 1.3.58 Evolutionary adaptation
- **1.1.21 Psychological adaptation** owns adaptation as a psychological process or pattern of adjustment.
- **1.3.58 Evolutionary adaptation** owns the evolutionary concept of heritable characteristics shaped by selection.
- Boundary: within-lifetime psychological adjustment vs population-level evolutionary adaptation.

### 1.1.12 Psychological organization ↔ 1.3.28 Brain organization
- **1.1.12 Psychological organization** owns organization of psychological functions, processes, states, and relationships.
- **1.3.28 Brain organization** owns anatomical and functional organization of the nervous system at the brain level.
- Boundary: psychological architecture vs biological architecture.

### 1.1.39 Psychological continuity ↔ 1.3 biological change
Psychological continuity remains a psychological-level concept. Biological continuity, neural plasticity, injury, development, and recovery can contribute to psychological continuity or disruption but do not replace its psychological ownership.

### 1.1.43–1.1.54 Explanation taxonomy ↔ 1.3 biological concepts
Chapter 1.1 owns the general explanatory distinctions: biological, psychological, social, cultural, multilevel, systems, dynamic-systems, functional, structural, process, and mechanistic explanation. Chapter 1.3 supplies biological subject matter that can be used within a biological or mechanistic explanation. It does not redefine the general explanation taxonomy.

### Chapter 1.2 scientific methods ↔ Chapter 1.3 biological phenomena
Chapter 1.2 owns how psychological and behavioral knowledge is produced, measured, evaluated, and justified scientifically. Chapter 1.3 owns the biological phenomena and methods specifically assigned to its ontology. Methodological concepts are not duplicated merely because Chapter 1.3 uses them.

## 1.9.1 Thinking

## 1. Purpose
Establish the meaning, function, and boundaries of **Thinking** within the representation architecture of Chapter 1.9.

## 2. Definition
Thinking is the coordinated psychological activity of manipulating, evaluating, transforming, and using information in ways that support representation, reasoning, problem solving, planning, and goal-directed action.

## 3. Core Understanding
Thinking is best understood as a specific component of representation, not as a synonym for thinking as a whole. Its operation depends on the information available, the representation being used, the person's prior knowledge, task demands, goals, and contextual constraints. In empirical work, performance attributed to Thinking can also reflect interacting processes such as attention, memory, language, motivation, or decision-making. The construct is therefore most informative when its defining operation is separated from neighboring processes and when claims about mechanism are kept proportional to the evidence.

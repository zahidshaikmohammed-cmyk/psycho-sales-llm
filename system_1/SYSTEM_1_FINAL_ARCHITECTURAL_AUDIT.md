## 1. Audit conclusion

The 991-concept index is broad and covers the standard introductory/core psychology backbone, but it is NOT yet a full-fledged canonical architecture.

The audit found three classes of problems:

1. Genuine coverage gaps.
2. Concepts that are currently treated as equal-level entries even though they are different ontological types.
3. Chapters that contain cross-domain applications or relationships that should not become independent canonical owners.

The correct action is NOT simply to add more concepts until the number becomes large. The ontology must first be made structurally correct.

## 2. External coverage check

APA's current National Standards organize psychology around scientific inquiry plus biological, cognition, development/learning, social/personality, and mental/physical health pillars. The standards explicitly emphasize interactions among psychological, biological, social, and cultural factors and individual differences. OpenStax Psychology 2e independently covers research, biopsychology, consciousness, sensation/perception, learning, thinking/intelligence, memory, development, motivation/emotion, personality, social psychology, I-O psychology, stress/health, disorders, and treatment.

The current PSYCHO SALES System 1 covers most of these foundational domains, but the audit identifies several areas that need explicit ownership or clearer treatment.

## 4. Structural corrections required

### A. Do not treat every named phenomenon as the same ontological level.
The final index should distinguish:
1. Domains
2. Constructs
3. Processes
4. Mechanisms
5. States
6. Behavioral phenomena
7. Individual differences
8. Theories/models
9. Measurement concepts
10. Research methods
11. Applications

### B. Separate constructs from relationships.
Examples:
- “Emotion and Memory” is a relationship, not a standalone construct.
- “Stress and Attention” is a relationship, not a standalone construct.
- “Personality and Behavior” is a relationship.
These should become relationship nodes or subtopics rather than being counted as independent psychological constructs.

### C. Separate theory/model ownership.
Examples:
- Dual-process theory
- Prospect theory
- Self-determination theory
- Expectancy-value theory
- Cognitive appraisal theories
- Social-cognitive theory
These should be explicitly classified as theories/models and cross-linked to the constructs they explain.

### D. Separate clinical applications from general mechanisms.
Do not let disorders or treatment re-own:
- memory
- emotion
- attention
- learning
- motivation
- personality
- decision-making

### E. Strengthen the distinction between 1.13, 1.15 and 1.19.
- Personal identity → 1.13
- Social identity → 1.15
- Cultural identity → 1.19

### F. Strengthen the distinction between 1.6, 1.11 and 1.18.
- Learning mechanisms → 1.6
- Motivation/self-regulation → 1.11
- Action execution/control → 1.18

### G. Reconsider 1.18 as a full chapter.
Keep it only if it becomes a genuine action-control architecture. Otherwise it should become an integration layer rather than a peer domain.

### H. Treatment is currently underrepresented.
A full psychology architecture should explicitly represent psychological intervention/treatment science somewhere. It can remain a major section of 1.17 or become a dedicated System 1 chapter if the project defines applied/clinical psychology as part of the foundation.

### I. Vocational/I-O/sport/forensic psychology should NOT automatically be added to System 1.
These are applied domains. They should be represented later as applications unless the System 1 scope is deliberately expanded. OpenStax explicitly treats I-O, health, sport/exercise, forensic, and clinical psychology as applied areas rather than basic psychological mechanisms.

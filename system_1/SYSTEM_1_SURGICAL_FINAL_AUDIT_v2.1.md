## Surgical additions made

### Motivation and self-regulation
Added **self-efficacy** to Chapter 1.12. APA describes it as a person's perceived capability to perform behaviors needed for desired outcomes and connects it to motivation and behavioral change. citeturn1search2turn1search6

### Development and attachment
Made **attachment** a canonical developmental owner in Chapter 1.15. Personality and relationship chapters now treat attachment style/adult attachment as manifestations and links rather than duplicate owners. APA's current attachment literature explicitly covers attachment from infancy through adulthood. citeturn1search0turn1search3

### Sexuality
Added **sexual desire, sexuality, sexual behavior, and sexual orientation** as universal psychological topics. APA treats sexuality as a major psychological domain spanning desire, behavior, identity/orientation, development, biology, and social/cultural context. citeturn2search5turn2search19

Added **grief, bereavement, mourning, and loss adaptation** to Chapter 1.18. Kept **prolonged grief disorder** separate as a clinical condition in Chapter 1.19. ### Addiction

## Relationship-node control

The architecture no longer treats combinations such as:

- emotion and memory
- stress and memory
- language and attention
- culture and perception
- motivation and context
- intelligence and education
- personality and behavior
- individual differences in memory
- cultural variation in emotion
- exercise and psychological function
- social influence on decisions

as new standalone constructs.

They are **relationship/application nodes** connected to their canonical owners.

This is important for LLM retrieval: the system can retrieve the relationship without creating two competing definitions of the same underlying construct.

## Theory/model control

Models remain first-class knowledge objects where appropriate, but are tracked separately from basic constructs.

Examples include:

- prospect theory
- expected utility theory
- dual-process accounts
- self-determination theory
- expectancy-value theory
- regulatory focus theory
- James–Lange theory
- Cannon–Bard theory
- cognitive appraisal theories
- social-cognitive theory
- trait models
- psychodynamic models
- attachment models
- developmental systems approaches
- diathesis–stress model
- stress appraisal models

A model explains or organizes constructs; it does not become a second definition of the construct.

## Clinical boundary

Clinical conditions remain separate from general psychological mechanisms.

For example:

- fear remains an emotional construct in 1.13
- anxiety as an emotional experience is linked from 1.13
- anxiety disorders are clinical conditions in 1.19
- learning mechanisms remain in 1.7
- clinical learning mechanisms are applications in 1.19/1.20

This prevents clinical terminology from silently taking ownership of universal psychology.

# 1. CORE RULE

A concept may appear in many chapters as an application, relationship, or dependency.

It may have only **one canonical owner**.

Example:

- Emotion is owned by Chapter 1.12.
- Chapter 1.7 may explain how emotion affects memory.
- Chapter 1.9 may explain how emotion affects decisions.
- Chapter 1.15 may explain emotion in social interaction.

Those are relationships, not new owners.

The encyclopedia must never fully redefine the same construct in two places.

# 28. IMPORTANT: NOT EVERY SIMILAR TERM SHOULD BE MERGED

Some apparent similarities are scientifically distinct.

Examples:

### Attention vs consciousness
Related, not identical.

### Automaticity vs unconscious processing
Related, not identical.

### Learning vs memory
Learning changes knowledge/behavior through experience; memory concerns retention and retrieval.

### Motivation vs emotion
They interact but are not the same construct.

### Personality vs individual differences
Personality is one major domain of individual differences; individual differences is broader.

### Personal identity vs social identity
Different referents.

### Social influence vs persuasion
Persuasion is one form of influence with a particular communicative/influence process; general social influence is broader.

### General resistance vs persuasion resistance vs sales objection
Different levels and contexts.

### Stress vs emotion
Stress is a broader response/process involving appraisal and demands; particular emotional states may occur during stress.

### Pain sensation vs chronic pain
Acute sensory pain and chronic health phenomena require different levels of treatment.

### Skill learning vs performance
Learning changes capability; performance is the observed expression of capability.

### Scientific theory vs psychological construct
A theory explains relationships among constructs; it is not itself the same kind of object.

# WRITER'S PRIMARY MISSION

Write the assigned Topic so that:

**A human can understand it completely.**
**An AI can retrieve it precisely.**
**The concept has one authoritative home.**
**Nothing important is omitted.**
**Nothing unnecessary is added.**

The goal is **maximum understanding with minimum necessary words**.

Do not try to make the Topic look comprehensive.

Make it **actually comprehensive**.

Depth comes from the amount of important knowledge covered, not from unnecessary length.

---

# NON-NEGOTIABLE WRITING LAWS

### 1. ONE AUTHORITATIVE HOME

Every concept, fact, mechanism, distinction, theory, or principle must have one authoritative location.

If another Topic owns the knowledge:

**do not explain it again.**

Reference it instead.

---

### 2. MINIMUM SUFFICIENT WORDS

Write the shortest version that preserves complete and accurate understanding.

If removing a sentence does not reduce correct understanding:

**remove it.**

If removing a sentence creates a meaningful misunderstanding or knowledge gap:

**keep it.**

---

### 3. NO ARTIFICIAL DEPTH

Never add:

- filler
- repeated explanations
- decorative academic language
- unnecessary history
- unnecessary examples
- manufactured complexity
- repeated conclusions
- generic statements merely to make the Topic longer

Every paragraph must contribute new understanding.

---

### 4. ADAPTIVE DEPTH

Not every Topic requires the same number of subsections.

Use only the knowledge blocks that genuinely apply.

A simple concept may be short.

A major psychological construct may require substantial depth.

Both can be complete.

---

### 5. HUMAN + AI DUAL AUDIENCE

Write so that:

- an intelligent person without psychology training can understand it;
- an LLM can identify individual facts and relationships;
- future AI systems can distinguish fact, theory, evidence, inference and uncertainty.

Use plain English.

Do not sacrifice scientific precision for simplicity.

---

### 6. SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Never present:

- a theory as established fact;
- correlation as causation;
- interpretation as certainty;
- speculation as evidence;
- an unresolved question as settled knowledge.

Where certainty differs, state the difference clearly.

---

### 7. CONCEPT OWNERSHIP

Before writing any substantive explanation, determine:

**What does this Topic own?**

**What does another Topic own?**

Anything belonging elsewhere must be deferred rather than duplicated.

---

### 8. NO SALES CONTAMINATION OF UNIVERSAL PSYCHOLOGY

For **System 1 — Human Psychology**, explain the psychological concept itself.

Do not redefine universal psychology through sales.

Sales application belongs downstream and must never distort the underlying psychological concept.

Where applicable, Section 4 may identify legitimate sales relevance without rewriting the universal concept around sales.

---

# PERMANENT KNOWLEDGE OBJECT STRUCTURE

Every Topic must use these six sections **in this exact order**.

---

# 1. PURPOSE

### Answer:

**Why does this Topic exist?**

Explain what understanding the reader should gain from this Topic.

Usually **1–2 short paragraphs or a few sentences**.

Do not explain the entire concept here.

Do not repeat the Definition.

---

# 2. DEFINITION

### Answer:

**What is this concept?**

Give the clearest, shortest accurate definition.

The definition must identify the concept precisely enough that it cannot easily be confused with a neighboring concept.

### Conceptual distinction

Include a **What It Is Not** distinction when failing to provide it could create meaningful conceptual confusion.

Do not manufacture a distinction merely to fill space.

---

# 3. CORE UNDERSTANDING

This is the main knowledge section.

Use only the blocks genuinely required by the Topic.

Do not force every block into every Topic.

## Available Knowledge Blocks

### Components / Dimensions

Use when the concept has meaningful internal parts, dimensions or elements.

Answer:

**What is it made of?**

---

### Types / Forms

Use when meaningful types, forms, or variants exist.

Answer:

**What different forms can it take?**

---

### Mechanism

Use when understanding how the concept operates is necessary.

Answer:

**How does it work?**

---

### Antecedents

Use when understanding what contributes to producing or developing the concept is important.

Answer:

**What contributes to its occurrence or formation?**

Do not imply causation unless the evidence supports a causal interpretation.

---

### Development

Use when the concept forms, changes, strengthens, weakens, or evolves over time.

Answer:

**How does it develop or change?**

---

### Consequences

Use when the concept has important consequences.

Answer:

**What follows from it?**

Separate established effects from associations or interpretations.

---

### Moderators / Boundary Conditions

Use when the concept behaves differently depending on circumstances.

Answer:

**What strengthens, weakens, changes, or limits it?**

---

### Damage / Repair

Use only when the concept can meaningfully be disrupted, damaged, restored, repaired, or rebuilt.

Answer:

**How can it break, and how can it recover?**

---

### Calibration

Use only when the concept has meaningful levels where too little, too much, or poorly calibrated expression matters.

Answer:

**What does appropriate versus inappropriate intensity look like?**

---

### Theory / Model

Use when major theories or models materially improve understanding.

Clearly distinguish:

**the concept itself**

from

**a theory or model explaining the concept.**

Never present a theory as established fact merely because it has a recognized name.

---

### Evidence Status

Use when evidence quality, strength, disagreement, or scientific uncertainty materially matters.

Clearly distinguish among:

- established findings
- empirical associations
- causal evidence
- theories/models
- interpretations/inferences
- contested findings
- unresolved questions

State the overall evidence strength plainly when appropriate.

---

### Open Questions

Use when meaningful uncertainty genuinely remains.

Do not manufacture disagreement.

If something is simply unknown, say that it is unknown.

---

### Misconceptions

Use when there is a common misunderstanding that could materially interfere with correct understanding.

Correct it directly and briefly.

---

### Related Concepts

Identify important neighboring or dependent concepts.

Do not re-explain those concepts.

Explain only the relationship necessary to understand the current Topic.

Where useful, distinguish relationships such as:

- related to
- depends on
- differs from
- contributes to
- influenced by
- contains
- part of
- contrasts with

---

### Examples

Use the **minimum number of examples required for correct understanding**.

Usually one strong example is enough.

Use additional examples only when they materially improve understanding, such as when:

- a contrast must be shown;
- an edge case matters;
- the concept is highly abstract;
- one example could create a misleading impression.

Examples support understanding.

They do not replace conceptual explanation.

---

# 4. RELATIONSHIP TO THIS CHAPTER

Explain why this Topic belongs in its specific Chapter.

Usually this should be brief.

Show the Topic's role within the Chapter's conceptual structure.

Do not repeat the Topic's full definition or Core Understanding.

### Sales Relevance

Include only when genuinely relevant to PSYCHO SALES's operational mission.

Explain how the established psychological concept appears in the sales environment.

**Do not rewrite or distort the universal psychological concept for sales.**

Sales relevance must be derived from the established concept.

### Ethical Boundary

Whenever Sales Relevance is included, clearly state the legitimate boundary of application.

PSYCHO SALES rejects:

- manipulation
- deception
- false urgency
- fearmongering
- misrepresentation
- psychological coercion

Application must remain consistent with:

- understanding
- transparency
- autonomy
- professional integrity
- ethical influence

---

# 5. BOUNDARIES

This section protects Concept Ownership.

## Owns

State the knowledge that this Topic is the authoritative location for.

Keep this precise.

Do not claim ownership of neighboring concepts merely because they are mentioned.

## Defers

Identify important concepts that this Topic touches but does not own.

Name the relevant Topic where possible.

Do not re-explain deferred concepts.

The purpose is to prevent conceptual duplication across the encyclopedia.

---

# 6. KEY PRINCIPLES

End with the smallest set of principles necessary to preserve the Topic's permanent understanding.

Every principle must already be supported by the Topic.

Do not introduce new information here.

Do not simply repeat the entire Topic.

Each principle should be concise and durable.

---

# ADAPTIVE DEPTH TEST

Before adding any knowledge block, ask these questions:

### 1. Does it genuinely exist for this concept?

If no → omit.

### 2. Does it add new understanding?

If no → omit.

### 3. Would omitting it create a meaningful knowledge gap or misunderstanding?

If no → omit.

### 4. Does another Topic already own it?

If yes → defer/reference it.

### 5. Is there enough reliable knowledge to state it accurately?

If no → do not invent an answer.

If the uncertainty itself is important, identify it as an Open Question or appropriately qualified evidence statement.

---

# DENSITY TEST

After writing each block, ask:

> **Can this be made shorter without reducing correct understanding?**

If yes:

**shorten it.**

Then ask:

> **Can it be made shorter without increasing ambiguity or creating a false impression?**

If yes:

**shorten it.**

Stop only when the text reaches the **minimum sufficient form**.

# SENTENCE STANDARD

Prefer:

**one idea → one sentence**

Use common words whenever they preserve the meaning.

Define necessary technical terms in plain English when first introduced.

Prefer concrete explanation before abstraction.

Avoid:

- academic ornament
- unnecessarily long sentences
- vague language
- unexplained jargon
- unnecessary qualifiers
- repeated conclusions

Simple language is required.

Simple thinking is not.

---

# EVIDENCE LANGUAGE

When making knowledge claims, preserve their epistemic status.

Use clear language such as:

**Definition**

> X means...

**Established finding**

> Research consistently shows...

**Empirical association**

> X is associated with...

**Causal evidence**

> Evidence supports the conclusion that X contributes to...

**Theory / Model**

> According to [theory/model]...

**Interpretation / Inference**

> This suggests...

**Contested / Unresolved**

> This remains debated...

> It is not yet known whether...

Do not use causal language unless the available evidence supports a causal interpretation under an appropriate scientific methodology.

# CONSTITUTION v2.0 — PROJECT-WIDE SEMANTIC UPGRADE

**Status: CANONICAL — LOCKED**

This section upgrades the permanent writer constitution from structural completeness to semantic completeness. It is binding for all future remediation and all future canonical chapters.

## Architecture remains authoritative

The locked ontology, Topic identifiers, ownership registry, and chapter architecture remain authoritative unless a formal architecture change is approved. This constitution does not authorize casual renaming, deletion, duplication, or expansion.

## Semantic completeness

Each Topic must contain the knowledge necessary for its own subject matter. Applicable dimensions include:

- precise identity and scope;
- what the concept is not;
- mechanism or process logic;
- components or dimensions;
- forms or variants;
- antecedents and consequences;
- temporal dynamics;
- boundary conditions and context dependence;
- individual and developmental variation;
- biological, social, and cultural relationships where relevant;
- measurement and operationalization;
- major paradigms;
- competing explanations;
- theory/model status;
- evidence status and uncertainty;
- limitations;
- misconceptions;
- neighboring concepts and conceptual contrasts;
- cross-chapter relationships;
- useful retrieval aliases.

Not every Topic requires every dimension. Omission must be semantically justified.

## Conceptual distinction gate

For every Topic, identify the nearest plausible neighboring concepts. Explain the distinctions necessary to prevent a competent reader or model from collapsing those concepts together.

## Mechanism gate

Where a Topic describes a process, mechanism, causal pathway, regulatory system, or functional operation, explain how it operates at the appropriate level. Never invent mechanisms or convert proposed mechanisms into established fact.

## Evidence gate

Preserve the difference among definition, observation, association, causal evidence, theory/model, interpretation, speculation, and unresolved question.

## Uncertainty gate

Uncertainty is knowledge. State meaningful scientific limits plainly. Do not manufacture certainty or controversy.

## Boundary gate

Every Topic must state what it owns and what it defers. Deferrals must be meaningful where important cross-chapter boundaries exist.

## Relationship gate

Important relationships are knowledge. Where relevant, distinguish relationships such as influence, prediction, mediation, moderation, constraint, enablement, dependence, temporal order, interaction, competition, dissociation, overlap, and distinction without creating duplicate construct ownership.

## Retrieval gate

Canonical Topics must remain retrievable from natural-language variants. Where useful, preserve synonyms, alternate terminology, historical terminology, abbreviations, and common retrieval variants. Aliases never create competing definitions.

## Anti-template law

No Topic may contain generic filler generated merely to satisfy the schema. Audit smells include literal placeholders, identical explanatory paragraphs, identical relationship paragraphs, identical key-principle sets, and generic chapter-membership prose that conveys no Topic-specific information.

## Scientific contamination law

System 1 describes universal psychology. Sales, persuasion, buyer behavior, conversion, negotiation, commercial tactics, or product strategy must not redefine a universal psychological construct. Applications remain downstream.

## Machine composability

The prose corpus is the human-readable layer. Future machine systems should also be able to represent Topic ID, ontology type, owner, aliases, parent/child relations, cross-links, relationship types, evidence status, uncertainty, theory/model status, measurement references, and clinical/application status.

## Seal gate

A Topic or chapter may be marked **LOCKED & SEALED** only after:

1. ontology match;
2. structural validation;
3. ownership validation;
4. semantic depth audit;
5. neighboring-concept distinction audit;
6. scientific-integrity/evidence audit;
7. relationship/retrieval audit;
8. anti-template audit;
9. cross-chapter boundary audit;
10. final validation record.

Structural completeness alone is never sufficient for a new seal or re-seal.

## Versioning

When semantic depth improves without changing the locked ontology, preserve Topic IDs and names, record the revision, increment the knowledge version, and retain the validation trail.

## Final quality law

> The encyclopedia is finished only when a competent human and a capable machine can use each Topic without hidden assumptions, while the system remains honest about what psychology does not yet know.

# PSYCHO SALES — CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE CONSTITUTION

## Status

**FINAL PROJECT CONSTITUTION — GOVERNING STANDARD**

This constitution governs the architecture, ontology, knowledge writing, validation, ownership, scientific integrity, and LLM-training suitability of PSYCHO SALES.

---

# 1. PURPOSE

PSYCHO SALES is a canonical psychological knowledge system for understanding sales through scientifically grounded psychology.

It is designed for two audiences at the same time:

1. Human readers.
2. Language models.

The system must therefore be easy to understand without becoming intellectually shallow.

## Core principle

> **Simple language. Deep thinking.**

A reader with weak English should be able to understand the basic meaning quickly.

A knowledgeable reader should still find meaningful psychological depth underneath that simple language.

An LLM should be able to retrieve the knowledge object and understand exactly what concept it represents, what it is related to, and what it does not own.

---

# 2. MASTER BUILD LAW

The project follows:

> **Architecture → Ontology → Ownership Audit → Lock → Knowledge Writing → Validation → Versioning**

Do not reverse this order.

Do not generate large amounts of knowledge before the ontology has been audited.

Do not permanently lock a topic whose ownership is unresolved.

---

# 3. ONE CONCEPT = ONE CANONICAL OWNER

This is the central ontology law.

Every important concept must have one canonical owner.

A concept may be referenced by many systems.

A concept may be applied in many contexts.

A concept may appear in examples throughout the encyclopedia.

But its canonical definition must live in one place.

## Example

System 1 owns:

**Motivation**

System 6 may contain:

**Buyer Motivation**

System 9 may contain:

**Sales Motivation**

These domain-specific topics can explain how motivation operates in their own context.

They must not silently redefine general motivation.

---

# 4. APPLICATION IS NOT OWNERSHIP

A later system may use an earlier concept without owning it.

Examples:

- System 2 can apply attention psychology.
- System 4 can apply emotion and cognition.
- System 6 can apply motivation and decision-making.
- System 9 can apply self-regulation and learning.
- System 11 can combine concepts from every system.

Application does not transfer canonical ownership.

---

# 5. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## System 1 — Human Psychology

General psychological mechanisms underlying thought, emotion, motivation, perception, learning, judgment, decision-making, behavior, and social functioning.

**Role:** Foundational psychology layer.

## System 2 — Cold Calling Psychology

Psychology specific to initiating unsolicited or minimally anticipated sales contact.

**Role:** Owns the psychological situation created by cold outreach.

## System 3 — Communication Psychology

Psychological processes governing interpersonal communication.

**Role:** Owns communication mechanisms.

## System 4 — Persuasion Psychology

Psychological processes through which messages, sources, contexts, and social forces influence attitudes, judgments, intentions, and behavior.

**Role:** Owns general persuasion and persuasion-specific resistance.

## System 5 — Negotiation Psychology

Psychology of parties managing partially aligned and partially competing interests while seeking agreement.

**Role:** Owns negotiation-specific preparation, interaction, strategies, tactics, ethics, and outcomes.

## System 6 — Buyer Psychology

Psychological processes specific to buying.

**Role:** Owns the buyer-side purchasing domain.

## System 7 — Objection & Resistance Psychology

Sales-specific psychological mechanisms behind objections, hesitation, rejection, defensiveness, and barriers to proceeding.

**Role:** Owns sales-specific objection and resistance phenomena.

## System 8 — Relationship Psychology

Psychological processes governing sustained buyer–seller relationships.

**Role:** Owns commercial relationship formation, maintenance, deterioration, repair, loyalty, and exit.

## System 9 — Sales Performance Psychology

Psychology of the salesperson as a performer.

**Role:** Owns salesperson motivation, self-regulation, confidence, attention, stress, resilience, habits, learning, and performance behavior.

## System 10 — Scientific Theories, Models & Frameworks

Scientific theories, models, frameworks, research methods, and formal explanatory structures used across PSYCHO SALES.

**Role:** Scientific synthesis layer.

## System 11 — PSYCHO SALES Intelligence

Integrated reasoning over Systems 1–10.

**Role:** Knowledge representation, relationship mapping, psychological diagnosis, state modeling, scenario reasoning, evidence handling, retrieval, and sales decision support.

System 11 must not create new psychological owners.

---

# 6. SYSTEM BOUNDARY RULE

Every system must answer four questions:

1. What does this system own?
2. What does it not own?
3. Which neighboring systems does it interact with?
4. Where does ownership stop?

If these questions cannot be answered clearly, the system is not ready to lock.

---

# 7. CHAPTER OWNERSHIP RULE

A chapter must represent a coherent domain.

A chapter must not exist merely because a concept is useful in sales.

Do not create duplicate chapters such as:

- General Motivation
- Sales Motivation
- Buyer Motivation

and then make all three general explanations of motivation.

Instead:

- System 1 owns the general construct.
- Later systems explain domain-specific manifestations.

---

# 8. TOPIC OWNERSHIP RULE

Every topic must have:

- one canonical name;
- one canonical owner;
- one defined meaning;
- clear boundaries;
- related concepts;
- a stable semantic identity.

Topic names should describe the actual concept.

Avoid artificial naming created only to fill a chapter.

---

# 9. CANONICAL TOPIC STANDARD

Every knowledge object should normally contain:

## 1. Purpose

Why the concept exists in the encyclopedia.

## 2. Definition

A short, precise explanation of what the concept means.

## 3. Core Understanding

The deeper explanation.

## 4. Mechanism

How the process works.

## 5. Conditions

What can cause, strengthen, weaken, or change it.

## 6. Effects

What the process can influence.

## 7. Relationship to This Chapter

Why the concept belongs here.

## 8. Related Concepts

Nearby concepts and their relationship.

## 9. Boundaries

What the concept does not mean and what it does not own.

## 10. Examples

Concrete examples that clarify the mechanism.

## 11. Scientific Status

Distinguish established evidence, theoretical explanation, empirical observation, practical interpretation, and unresolved questions where relevant.

## 12. Key Principles

The most important conclusions.

The structure may be shortened or expanded when scientifically necessary.

Sections must never be added merely to increase length.

---

# 10. LANGUAGE CONSTITUTION

Use very simple English.

Prefer:

- short sentences;
- familiar words;
- direct verbs;
- one idea at a time;
- clear cause → process → effect explanations;
- concrete examples.

Avoid:

- unnecessary academic vocabulary;
- decorative writing;
- inflated language;
- vague statements;
- unnecessarily long sentences;
- repeated ideas.

## Critical rule

**Simple does not mean shallow.**

The language should be simple.

The thinking should be deep.

---

# 11. DEPTH CONSTITUTION

A major concept should explain, where relevant:

- what it is;
- why it exists;
- what causes it;
- how it works;
- what changes it;
- what it affects;
- how it interacts with other mechanisms;
- what it does not mean;
- where its boundaries are;
- why it matters.

Do not confuse length with depth.

Depth comes from explaining mechanism and relationships.

---

# 12. CUMULATIVE TEACHING

The knowledge must teach progressively.

The preferred movement is:

> **Definition → Mechanism → Conditions → Interaction → Consequence → Application**

Every paragraph should add new understanding.

If a paragraph only repeats the previous paragraph using different words, remove it.

---

# 13. SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Never invent:

- studies;
- statistics;
- citations;
- experiments;
- psychological mechanisms;
- scientific consensus.

Distinguish clearly between:

- established findings;
- strong empirical evidence;
- theoretical explanations;
- empirical observations;
- practical interpretations;
- contested findings;
- unresolved questions.

When evidence is uncertain, the writing must show that uncertainty.

---

# 14. EXAMPLE INTEGRITY

Examples exist to make a concept easier to understand.

An example must not accidentally redefine the concept.

An example must not introduce a neighboring concept as though it were part of the canonical definition.

Examples should show mechanism, not merely decorate the page.

---

# 15. CROSS-SYSTEM REFERENCES

When a topic depends on another concept:

1. name the related concept;
2. explain only the amount needed;
3. state the relationship;
4. link to the canonical owner when possible;
5. stop before taking over the neighboring concept.

This protects One Concept = One Owner.

---

# 16. SCIENTIFIC MODEL RULE

A theory, model, framework, or research method is not automatically the owner of the psychological construct it explains.

System 10 owns scientific models as knowledge objects.

The underlying construct keeps its canonical owner.

---

# 17. INTELLIGENCE LAYER RULE

System 11 can:

- connect concepts;
- compare mechanisms;
- diagnose psychological situations;
- model states;
- reason across systems;
- retrieve relevant knowledge;
- construct explanations.

System 11 cannot:

- create a competing owner;
- silently redefine a canonical concept;
- override scientific evidence;
- turn a practical sales tactic into an established psychological law.

---

# 18. SALES APPLICATION RULE

PSYCHO SALES may explain practical sales applications.

However:

> Practical usefulness is not scientific proof.

A sales technique may be useful without being a universal psychological law.

The encyclopedia must separate mechanism from sales folklore.

---

# 19. MANIPULATION AND ETHICS

The encyclopedia may describe influence, persuasion, pressure, resistance, tactics, and manipulation mechanisms for understanding.

Understanding a mechanism does not automatically mean endorsing its use.

Where ethical boundaries are relevant, they should be stated clearly.

The knowledge system should support informed and responsible use of psychological knowledge.

---

# 20. LLM TRAINING CONSTITUTION

Every knowledge object must be suitable for independent retrieval.

An LLM should be able to determine:

- what the concept is;
- what it means;
- what mechanism it describes;
- what causes or changes it;
- what it affects;
- what it is related to;
- who owns it;
- what it is not;
- how confident the evidence is.

Do not optimize for keyword density.

Optimize for semantic clarity.

Do not use rhetorical filler to create apparent depth.

Use explicit relationships and boundaries.

---

# 21. RETRIEVAL STABILITY

Terminology must remain stable.

If two terms mean different things, keep them separate.

If two terms are synonyms, explain the relationship instead of creating unnecessary duplicate concepts.

Canonical names should remain stable across files, systems, examples, and references.

---

# 22. KNOWLEDGE OBJECT INDEPENDENCE

A knowledge object should be understandable when retrieved without the surrounding chapter.

It may depend on neighboring concepts, but those dependencies should be explicit.

The object must not require hidden context to understand its core meaning.

---

# 23. COMPLETENESS TEST

A topic is not complete merely because it has a definition.

Before acceptance, ask:

1. Can a beginner explain what it means?
2. Can the reader explain how it works?
3. Can the reader identify important conditions?
4. Can the reader distinguish it from nearby concepts?
5. Is the canonical owner clear?
6. Has the topic avoided stealing another concept's ownership?
7. Does every major paragraph add new understanding?
8. Is scientific confidence represented honestly?
9. Is the topic independently retrievable?
10. Does the topic belong in this exact chapter and system?

A critical failure requires revision.

---

# 24. ONTOLOGY AUDIT

Before a system is locked:

- audit every chapter;
- audit every topic;
- compare neighboring topics;
- compare earlier systems;
- search for duplicate owners;
- search for renamed duplicates;
- search for overly broad topics;
- verify boundaries;
- verify scientific terminology.

If ownership conflicts exist, resolve them before knowledge generation.

---

# 25. LOCKING RULE

A system may be marked **LOCKED** only after:

1. its chapter ontology is complete;
2. its topic ontology is complete;
3. ownership has been audited;
4. cross-system conflicts have been checked;
5. boundaries have been documented;
6. scientific terminology has been reviewed;
7. the ontology passes the completion tests.

A locked system can still be versioned later if new evidence requires change.

Locking means controlled stability, not permanent refusal to improve.

---

# 26. VERSIONING RULE

Changes must be traceable.

Major ontology changes require:

- updated version information;
- reason for change;
- affected concepts;
- ownership impact;
- validation.

Never silently change a canonical definition when the change affects meaning.

---

# 27. QUALITY GATE

No knowledge object enters the canonical corpus unless it passes:

**Clarity + Depth + Ownership + Boundaries + Scientific Integrity + Retrieval Stability**

All six matter.

A beautiful explanation with bad ownership is a failure.

A scientifically accurate explanation that a beginner cannot understand is also a failure.

---

# 28. FINAL PROJECT STANDARD

PSYCHO SALES must always aim for:

> **Simple language. Deep psychology. Precise ownership. Honest science. Strong boundaries. Stable knowledge. LLM-ready meaning.**

That is the permanent standard for the project.

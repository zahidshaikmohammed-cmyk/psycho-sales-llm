# PSYCHO SALES — CANONICAL WRITING CONSTITUTION

## Purpose

Every knowledge object must be scientifically meaningful, psychologically deep, extremely easy to understand, and structurally useful for language-model training.

## Core rule

**Simple language. Deep thinking.**

The reader should understand the first meaning quickly, while the deeper paragraphs explain the mechanism underneath it.

## One Concept = One Owner

A topic may reference neighboring concepts, but it must not silently take ownership of them.

When a neighboring concept is necessary for understanding:
- introduce only the amount needed;
- identify its relationship to the current concept;
- stop before explaining the neighboring concept in full.

## Writing standard

Every topic should normally contain:

1. Purpose
2. Definition
3. Core Understanding
4. Relationship to This Chapter
5. Boundaries
6. Key Principles

The exact structure may expand when scientifically necessary, but sections must never be added merely to increase length.

## Language

Use plain, direct English.

Prefer:
- short sentences
- familiar words
- one idea at a time
- concrete examples
- explicit cause → process → effect explanations

Avoid:
- unnecessary academic jargon
- decorative language
- inflated vocabulary
- vague claims
- long sentences carrying many unrelated ideas

The target is a reader with weak English who can grasp the basic meaning at first glance.

## Depth

Easy language does not mean shallow content.

For each major idea, explain where useful:
- what it is
- what causes it
- how it works
- what changes it
- what it affects
- how it interacts with nearby concepts
- what it does not mean
- why it matters

Do not repeat the same point using different words.

## Cumulative teaching

Each paragraph should add new understanding.

The explanation should move from:
definition → mechanism → conditions → interaction → consequence → application.

## Scientific integrity

Do not present speculation as established fact.

Distinguish:
- established findings
- theoretical explanations
- empirical observations
- practical interpretations
- unresolved questions

Do not invent studies, statistics, citations, mechanisms, or scientific consensus.

## Examples

Examples should clarify the concept.

They must not accidentally introduce a second concept as if it were part of the owner's definition.

## LLM training suitability

Every topic must be:
- self-contained enough to retrieve independently;
- explicit about its canonical concept;
- explicit about related concepts;
- explicit about boundaries;
- semantically consistent in terminology;
- free from unnecessary rhetorical filler.

Do not optimize for keyword stuffing.

Optimize for conceptual clarity and stable meaning.

## Completion test

Before a topic is accepted, ask:

1. Can a beginner explain what the concept means?
2. Can the reader explain how it works?
3. Can the reader distinguish it from nearby concepts?
4. Is the canonical owner clear?
5. Has the topic avoided stealing another topic's ownership?
6. Does every major paragraph add new understanding?
7. Is the scientific confidence level represented honestly?
8. Could an LLM retrieve the topic without confusing it with neighboring concepts?

A topic that fails a critical test is revised before the system proceeds.
